# Building in Public

## Idea
Share the process of making something while you make it, not just the finished result, so the work earns trust, feedback and an audience before it ships.

## Definition
Building in public means documenting the work as it happens: what you're making, what broke, what you learned, sometimes the numbers. It turns the long, invisible middle of a project into a stream of small, honest updates. It pays off in three ways. People who watch you build come to trust you, so an audience exists before launch. Feedback arrives while the work can still change. And having said in public that you'll make the thing pushes you to keep going. The cost is real too: you have to be comfortable showing unfinished, sometimes bad work, and you're open to criticism before you're ready. It's teaching what you just learned, not performing expertise.

## Source
Austin Kleon gave it its most quoted form in *[[Show Your Work]]* (2014): "become a documentarian of what you do." Buffer's open-salary and revenue transparency (from 2013) and the indie-hacker movement around 2019–2020 made "build in public" a startup norm. Robin Sloan's phrase "working with the garage door up", picked up by Andy Matuschak, describes the same habit for personal work.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the practice that [[Show Your Work]] argues for, and it rests on the idea in [[A Draft, Not a Verdict]]: a rough draft shared in public is part of the process, not a judgment on you.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Sharing often turns into [[Consistency]], and over time the stream of updates becomes a [[Digital Garden]]: a public body of work that grows in the open. It is also the most direct way to put [[You Can Build Stuff]] into practice.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[The Protégé Effect]] explains why it helps the builder too: explaining what you just learned deepens it. [[Explain in Layers]] is a way to make those updates readable to people at different levels.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Building in public can turn into performing in public, where the updates become the product and the work stalls. [[Deep Work]] pulls the other way, since the best work often needs long stretches with no audience at all.
