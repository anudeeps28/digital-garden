# Explain in Layers

## Idea
Don't guess your audience's level and pitch once. Write the same thing three times, plainest first, and let each reader stop where they've had enough.

## Definition
Layered explanation presents one piece of work at increasing depth: a plain-language version using only everyday objects and no jargon; a middle version that names the real concepts and the actual decisions; and a technical version with the specifics — file names, configuration, exact failure modes. Each layer is complete on its own, so a reader who stops after the first has a true picture rather than a partial one, and a reader who continues gets resolution rather than repetition. It works because the usual alternative — one explanation aimed at an imagined average reader — is simultaneously too dense for the manager and too vague for the engineer. It also functions as a test of your own understanding: the plain layer is impossible to write convincingly if you don't actually know what happened, because there's no jargon left to hide behind.

## Source
Popularized by WIRED's *5 Levels* video series (2017-); the underlying idea that an explanation must be reconstructible in simple terms is the Feynman technique, and progressive disclosure is its interface-design cousin.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It rests on the claim that understanding and the ability to simplify are the same skill — the premise behind [[Clarity In, Clarity Out]].

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Writing the plain layer first exposes the gaps in your own model before you present anything, which makes it a preparation tool, not just a formatting one.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Scott Adams' Four Rules of Writing]] optimizes a single layer for clarity; [[Show Your Work]] is the same generosity applied to process.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Three layers is three times the writing, and for a genuinely homogeneous audience it's wasted effort. Analogies also leak — the plain layer can leave a reader confidently holding a wrong model that the deeper layers never get the chance to correct.
