# Scott Adams' Four Rules of Writing

## Idea
Good writing isn't talent — it's four mechanical rules anyone can apply: simplify, hook, shorten, activate.

## Definition
Scott Adams distilled effective writing into a 250-word essay called "The Day You Became A Better Writer." Naval Ravikant calls it the best writing resource he knows. The four rules: (1) **Simplicity** — remove adverbs, use shorter words, eliminate redundancy; say "happy" instead of "very happy." (2) **Strong opening** — hook the reader from the very first sentence; curiosity is the most powerful force in writing. (3) **Concise sentences** — one thought per sentence, no jargon, no flowery language; readers process information better when it's bite-sized. (4) **Active voice** — put the subject before the action; "The boy hit the ball" beats "The ball was hit by the boy." The meta-insight: these rules work because writing isn't about sounding smart — it's about being understood.

## Source
Scott Adams, "The Day You Became A Better Writer" (blog post); recommended by Naval Ravikant on The Tim Ferriss Show; popularized by Dickie Bush (@dickiebush) on X.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This belongs to the broader question of why writing matters at all, and Naval's answer is that "ideas are the new oil" — writing is how you extract, refine, and clarify raw ideas. The deeper root is that [[Writing Clarifies Thinking]]: you don't write because you have clear ideas, you write to make your ideas clear.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
These four rules connect directly to [[Show Your Work]] — if you can write clearly, sharing your process becomes dramatically easier. They also enable [[Building in Public]], where concise, clear writing is the medium through which you attract an audience and collaborators.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Charisma]] is the spoken version of the same principle — warmth (clarity, accessibility) plus competence (substance, precision). [[AI Removes the Barriers]] neighbors this too: AI can help you write, but taste and clarity of thought remain human skills.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Academic and corporate writing culture rewards the opposite of these rules — complexity, passive voice, and hedging signal "seriousness." The rules also clash with the idea that great writing requires suffering or innate talent; Adams' point is that it's mechanical and learnable, which is liberating but also deflating to romantics.
