# Good Theft vs Bad Theft

## Idea
Every creator borrows. The difference is whether you study, credit and transform many sources, or skim and copy one.

## Definition
Nothing is fully original, so the useful question isn't whether you borrowed but how. Kleon draws the line with pairs. Good theft **honors** the source, **studies** it deeply, **steals from many**, **credits** where ideas came from, **transforms** what it takes, and **remixes** it into something new. Bad theft **degrades**, **skims**, **steals from one**, **plagiarizes**, **imitates**, and **rips off**. Copying one source is plagiarism. Copying many and combining them is research and, eventually, style. The aim is to understand why your heroes made their choices so you can think like them, not to reproduce their surface. Your own style starts in the places where your copying fails.

## Source
Austin Kleon, *[[Steal Like an Artist]]* (2012), principle #1, with his "good theft vs bad theft" chart. The older version is T. S. Eliot's "Immature poets imitate; mature poets steal; bad poets deface what they take, and good poets make it into something better, or at least something different" (*The Sacred Wood*, 1920). "Good artists copy, great artists steal" is widely misattributed to Picasso and was popularized by Steve Jobs.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It rests on [[You Are Your Training Data]]: you become a mix of what you take in, so choose your influences carefully and widely.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Stealing well over years builds [[Taste Is Earned Conviction]]. Crediting your sources openly is part of [[Building in Public]] and of [[Show Your Work]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[We Learn by Doing, Not by Watching]] covers the same apprenticeship: you learn by copying the craft with your own hands, not by admiring it. [[Tag Claims as Cited or Assumed]] is the same honesty about where an idea came from, applied to analysis.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
With generative AI, the line is harder to draw: a model can "steal from many" at a scale and with an opacity no single artist could, and whether that counts as transformation is unsettled. Some fields also guard against borrowing for good reason, such as patents, academic citation rules and trademarks.
