# Retention-Weighted Editing

## Idea
Concentrate your heaviest production effort where attention is most fragile and most valuable — the opening and the main argument — instead of spreading effects evenly across the whole video.

## Definition
Not every second of a video is worth the same. On YouTube, the steepest drop in the retention curve happens in the first 10–20 seconds, and the first 15–30 seconds decide whether a viewer commits to the rest. So editing effort should be weighted, not uniform: ambitious, illustrative effects front-loaded into the first few minutes and stacked again at the key argument, with lighter treatment everywhere else. Two rules keep it from tipping into noise. First, pacing should tighten in the intro (a visual reset every 10–20 seconds) then widen once the viewer is hooked (cuts every 25–40 seconds) — over-editing tires people out. Second, every visual should illustrate the *exact* thing being said in that moment; generic B-roll is the default an editor reaches for when nobody has told them precisely what to build.

## Source
Anudeep Sharma's editing spec for his YouTube channel (a "retention-weighted" brief for his video editor, July 2026). Reinforced by YouTube retention research: the sharpest audience drop-off clusters around seconds 10–20, and front-loading value beats a branded cold open.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This is the [[Pareto Principle]] applied to a timeline — a small slice of the video (the opening seconds and the core argument) drives most of the retention outcome, so a disproportionate share of effort belongs there. It answers a producer's version of "where does effort actually count?" rather than treating all footage as equal.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Weighting effort by retention forces a specific brief: name the trigger moments — a stat, a contrarian claim, a multi-step concept — and design the effect to hit exactly on the word. That discipline is what turns a vague "make it look good" into something an editor can execute, and it compounds with [[Volume Negates Luck]] — more videos, each with a sharp front end, widens the surface area for a hit.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Show Your Work]] shares the underlying instinct that presentation is part of the craft, not an afterthought. [[Scott Adams' Four Rules of Writing]] is the written-word cousin — grab attention early, cut what doesn't earn its place. Both say the first move has to work before the rest matters.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Taken too far, retention-weighting becomes manipulation — an over-edited barrage of zooms and whooshes that impresses other editors but exhausts the actual audience, or a hook that oversells and gets punished when the body underdelivers. The counter-pressure is honesty: the front-loaded promise has to match what the video actually delivers, or the retention you bought in second five collapses by minute two.
