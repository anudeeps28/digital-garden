# Clarity In, Clarity Out

## Idea
The quality of what you get — from AI or from people — is directly proportional to the clarity of what you put in.

## Definition
Everyone assumes that as AI gets smarter, you can get lazier with your questions. It's the opposite. A great model will confidently nail the wrong question. The smarter it gets, the more your question matters — not less. "Plan me a trip to Japan" gets a generic answer anyone could have written. "Five days in Tokyo — I love food, hate crowds, two hundred a day, no temples" gets something sharp and personal. Same AI, same capability. The only thing that changed was the question. And off the screen, this is identical with people — a vague ask gets a vague answer. The difference is that a person will politely fake it, while AI won't. That's the gift: AI is an honest mirror for how clearly you're actually thinking.

## Source
Echoes the computing principle "Garbage In, Garbage Out" (GIGO), attributed to IBM programmer George Fuechsel (c. 1957). The life-skill framing — "the quality of your questions determines the quality of your answers" — was popularized by Tony Robbins. AI-specific articulation from Anudeep Sharma's "Models & Mindsets #1" video (June 2026).

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This is a specific instance of [[Mental Clarity and Meaning]] — clarity of thought is the prerequisite to getting value from any tool or relationship. It also sits directly upstream of [[Prompt Engineering]]: the entire discipline of prompt design is the technical formalization of "be specific about what you want."

**Paths** — *where this leads*
If AI exposes unclear thinking, it becomes a training tool for [[Mental Clarity and Meaning]] itself — every bad AI output is feedback on your own vagueness. This also connects to [[AI Removes the Barriers]]: the barriers to creation are gone, but the barrier of unclear thinking remains, and it's now the bottleneck.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Prompt Engineering]] is the technical sibling — same principle applied to crafting system prompts and structured inputs for [[LLM (Large Language Model)|LLMs]]. [[Define Contract Before Implementation]] captures the same idea in software: specify what you want before you build, or you'll build the wrong thing.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
The counterargument is that truly great AI should handle ambiguity — should ask clarifying questions, infer intent, and fill in gaps. Future models may close this gap. But even then, the person who thinks clearly will get there faster, because they skip the clarification round-trips entirely.
