# The Four Gatekeepers

## Summary
A framework for why most people never achieve what they want: four sequential barriers (gatekeepers) that filter out the majority at each stage. The creator illustrates this with research showing that of 1,000 people who set New Year's resolutions, only 27 remain after one year, and by two years just one person is left.

## Key Ideas
- **Gatekeeper 1: Comfort ("Good Enough")** — Most people's current life is just good enough that the short-term dip required to change feels worse than staying put. Nobody is lazy when the building is on fire. The rule: chase greatness, not good enough.
- **Gatekeeper 2: The Grind** — Persistence through the long, boring middle. 25% quit within a week, 50% by month one. The grind kills basically everyone because no one can stomach it.
- **Primordial Resilience** — Corner someone with no way out and you'll see what they're capable of. Humans can chase antelopes across savannahs until they collapse from exhaustion — that ability is still in you.
- The math: 700 of 1,000 people don't even start. Of the 300 who do, most quit within months. Simply showing up and persisting puts you in rarefied company.

## Quotes / Excerpts
> "Laziness is just a label people give themselves because it stops the conversation and clears them of responsibility."

> "No one is lazy when they find themselves stuck in a burning building."

## My Thoughts
The "good enough" trap is the most insidious gatekeeper because it feels reasonable. The math of persistence (1,000 → 27 → 1) makes the case for consistency better than any motivational speech.
