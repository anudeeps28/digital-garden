# Slow Productivity

## Idea
Do fewer things, work at a natural pace, and obsess over quality — the opposite of measuring output by volume.

## Definition
Cal Newport's framework pushes back against the modern knowledge-work default of measuring productivity by busyness — more tasks, more meetings, more PRs, more visible output. Slow Productivity rests on three principles: (1) **Do fewer things** — reduce your active commitments so you can give real attention to what remains. Pseudo-productivity (looking busy) is the enemy. (2) **Work at a natural pace** — sustained intensity burns out; variable pacing across days, weeks, and seasons produces more over time. (3) **Obsess over quality** — when you do fewer things, the things you do can be genuinely excellent. This isn't about being lazy — it's about recognizing that meaningful output requires depth, and depth requires margin. In the AI era, this matters more: AI can generate volume instantly, so human value shifts entirely to the quality layer.

## Source
Cal Newport, "Slow Productivity: The Lost Art of Accomplishment Without Burnout" (2024), named Economist Best Book of 2024. Extends ideas from his earlier "Deep Work" (2016) and "Digital Minimalism" (2019).

## Compass

**Roots** — This grows from [[The Daily Highlight]] (protecting one thing that matters) and [[The Eisenhower Matrix]] (ruthlessly filtering what deserves your time), and is the philosophical parent of [[The Exit Condition]] — defining "done" before starting is slow productivity in action.

**Paths** — Practicing slow productivity leads to [[Consistency Over Intensity]] (natural pacing over heroic sprints), and its quality obsession connects to [[Retention-Weighted Editing]] — spending effort on the parts that actually matter rather than polishing everything equally.

**Neighbors** — This lives near [[Toxic Productivity]] (the failure mode it diagnoses), [[Kaizen]] (another system that values consistent small steps over volume), and [[Selective Intelligence]] (choosing where to apply cognitive effort).

**Clash** — [[Volume Negates Luck]] and [[The Parable of the Pottery Class]] argue the opposite: sometimes you need raw output volume to find quality, especially early in a skill. The resolution is stage-dependent — volume when learning, slow productivity when performing.
