# Only the Rich Will Burn Candles

## Idea
When a technology takes over a job, the old way does not vanish; it becomes a boutique pleasure, and cheap AI will do this to thinking for yourself.

## Definition
Edison predicted electricity so cheap that only the rich would burn candles. He was right, but not as he meant it: candles kept the nice part of their job and became expensive objects bought for atmosphere. The same pattern applies to unaided thought. Working something out yourself is becoming an announced act, like baking bread or shooting film. The catch is that boutique means optional, and something you only do when you feel like it is something you mostly do not do. We are about to learn how much of our thinking was ever really ours, and how much was just the only option available.

## Source
Personal reflection on Edison's quote. Quote Investigator traces the documented original to a New York Herald report of 4 January 1880, where Edison said "none but the extravagant will burn tallow candles"; the "rich" wording was attributed to him by 1914.
