# The God of a Small World

## Idea
People don't fall in love with programming for the salary — they fall in love with the feeling of commanding a small universe to do exactly what they want.

## Definition
At some point, you touched a computer, and the computer did what you wanted. You made it do what you wanted it to do, and you realized that you were a god — a small god in a very small world. That moment of agency — of typing instructions and watching a machine obey — is what hooks people on programming. It's not the money, not the job security, not the LinkedIn clout. It's the raw creative power of making something from nothing and watching it work. This is why the best programmers often code outside of work, why side projects exist, and why the salary explanation never quite captures why someone stays in tech for decades.

## Source
Unattributed speech circulated via Instagram reel (June 2026); the sentiment echoes what many programmers describe as their origin story — the moment they wrote their first program and it *worked*.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This is a specific case of [[Intrinsic Motivation]] — the psychological principle that people perform best and persist longest when driven by internal satisfaction rather than external rewards. The parent question is what [[Happiness]] actually consists of: this note argues it's agency and creation, not compensation.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
If the hook is the feeling of creation, then [[AI Removes the Barriers]] amplifies it enormously — now you can be a small god in a much larger world, building things that used to require entire teams. This also feeds [[Show Your Work]]: when you love the act of building, sharing what you build becomes natural rather than performative.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[You Cannot Compete with Someone Who Is Having Fun]] is the competitive corollary — someone programming for the joy of creation will outwork someone programming for the paycheck every time. [[We Learn by Doing, Not by Watching]] captures the same truth from a learning angle: the feeling only comes from actually building, never from watching tutorials.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
[[Toxic Productivity]] is the dark side — the same love of building can become compulsive, crowding out rest and relationships. And the salary-first crowd has a point: not everyone has the privilege of choosing work for joy. For many people, the paycheck is the point, and that's valid — this note describes the feeling, not a prescription.
