# A Draft, Not a Verdict

## Idea
Your first attempt is raw material to iterate on, not a final judgment on your ability — so regenerate instead of quitting.

## Definition
Nobody deletes ChatGPT after one bad answer. You refine the prompt, open a new session, and hit regenerate until the output is good, because you treat a first output as a draft, not a verdict. Yet people apply the opposite standard to themselves: one bad workout, one rejected application, one project that flopped, and they take it as proof of a permanent limit. This is a double standard. Every skill you have was built from reps that felt like failure while you were doing them — the LeetCode pattern that only stuck after ten failed attempts, the hundred practice problems that finally installed a way of thinking. Nobody counts those attempts because failing doesn't feel like progress, but it *is* the progress. The move is to grade yourself on the trajectory across many attempts, not on attempt one.

## Source
Anudeep Sharma's "Models & Mindsets #3 — Regenerate: your first try isn't final" (July 2026), using the LLM regenerate button as a metaphor for iteration. The underlying science is Carol Dweck's *growth mindset* (ability is malleable, failure is data) paired with Anders Ericsson's *deliberate practice* (skill is built through feedback-driven reps, not innate talent).

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This sits under the growth-mindset question of whether ability is fixed or built, and it borrows its metaphor from the AI world — the [[LLM (Large Language Model)]] regenerate loop, where a mediocre first output is simply the start of an iteration, never the ceiling. It's the personal-development twin of how you already treat the tools you use every day.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Believing attempt one is only a draft is what makes [[Consistency Over Intensity]] and [[Discipline Over Motivation]] livable — you keep showing up because no single rep is a verdict. It leads straight to [[Volume Negates Luck]]: pile up enough regenerations and good outcomes stop being luck and start being statistics.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[The Parable of the Pottery Class]] is the closest kin — the students who made a pot a day (many drafts) beat the ones chasing one perfect pot (one verdict). [[We Learn by Doing, Not by Watching]] and [[The Winner Has Failed More Than the Loser Has Tried]] tell the same story: mastery is an accumulation of attempts, most of which felt like failing. [[Errare Humanum Est]] and [[Fail Fast Fail Loudly]] treat error as normal input, not final output.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Perfectionism is the direct opponent — it reads attempt one as a verdict and would rather not try than produce a bad draft. There's also a real edge case: regenerating forever can become a way to avoid shipping, so [[Fail Fast Fail Loudly]] supplies the missing constraint — iterate, but in public and on a clock, not in private indefinitely.
