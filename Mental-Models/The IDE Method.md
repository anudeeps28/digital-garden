# The IDE Method

## Idea
A three-step system for learning anything fast: Index the landscape, Drill with practice problems, Evolve by recalibrating what to learn next.

## Definition
**Index** — Before learning anything, spend one hour surveying the landscape. Map all the concepts, subtopics, and relationships without going deep. Use docs, YouTube overviews, or ask an LLM "What are the main concepts here?" You're not learning yet — you're building a map of what exists. **Drill** — Pick one topic from your index and do 30-50 practice problems. Not tutorials. Not reading. Problems. When stuck, look up the answer, understand the pattern, close it, and solve it again without looking. The struggle isn't a bug — it's your brain building neural pathways. By problem 30-40, patterns emerge and things click. **Evolve** — Regularly reassess *what* you're drilling. What a junior needs (DSA, syntax) is different from what a mid-level needs (system design) or a senior (architecture, leadership). Use AI to audit: "Given my experience, what should I focus on next?" Index tells you what to learn in a topic. Drill makes you actually learn it. Evolve ensures you're learning the right topics for your stage.

## Source
Anudeep Sharma's framework (YouTube, August 2026). The Index step draws on concept mapping research. The Drill step is grounded in Anders Ericsson's deliberate practice research (1993) and Cal Newport's "Deep Work" (2016). The Evolve step applies growth-stage thinking from software engineering career progression models.

## Compass

**Roots** — This is rooted in [[We Learn by Doing, Not by Watching]] (drilling > tutorials) and [[Familiar, Not Easy]] (the struggle of drilling is just unfamiliarity being converted to familiarity through reps).

**Paths** — The IDE Method leads to [[Volume Negates Luck]] outcomes — 30-50 problems per concept produces the pattern recognition that makes you fast, and connects to [[The Paradox of Freedom]] by building the foundational fluency that enables creative application.

**Neighbors** — This lives near [[85% Rule]] (calibrating drill difficulty for optimal learning) and [[Consistency Over Intensity]] (daily drilling sessions compound faster than weekend marathons).

**Clash** — The method is optimized for skill-based learning (coding, math, technical domains) and may not apply well to domains where pattern recognition matters less than taste, judgment, or creativity — areas where [[A Draft, Not a Verdict]] and exploratory thinking matter more.
