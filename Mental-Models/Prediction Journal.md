# Prediction Journal

## Idea
Write down what you think will happen and how sure you are, then go back and check — the gap between the two is the only honest measure of your judgment.

## Definition
A prediction journal is a running log of forecasts with explicit confidence attached: not "I think this launch goes well" but "70% chance we ship by March". The point is not accuracy. It is calibration — learning whether your 70% means 70%. Without the written record, memory quietly edits itself: you remember having expected whatever happened, so no judgment is ever disconfirmed and no learning occurs. Writing the number down before the outcome removes the edit. It is the cheapest instrument there is for finding out whether your confidence is earned.

## Source
Captured from my own reel on habits for the AI age (September 2026) — and the one habit on that list I admitted to not having started.

The research behind it is Philip Tetlock's. His 2005 work found expert political forecasters barely beat chance and were badly miscalibrated. He then co-founded the Good Judgment Project in 2011, which competed in the IARPA forecasting tournament; the best amateur "superforecasters" outperformed professional intelligence analysts with classified access by roughly 30%. Tetlock and Dan Gardner wrote it up as *Superforecasting* (2015). The traits that separated them were ordinary: thinking in probabilities, updating in small increments, and owning errors out loud.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This belongs to the family of practices that make thinking checkable rather than merely confident, which is the core of [[Critical Thinking Skills]] — judgment and questioning are useless if you never find out whether yours were any good. It is also one of the concrete trainable capacities behind [[Train What Can't Be Handed Over]].

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Kept honestly it produces a tolerance for being wrong in public, which is what [[Errare Humanum Est]] treats as the normal mechanism of learning rather than a failure. It makes every forecast a revisable artifact in the way [[A Draft, Not a Verdict]] describes.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It is the personal version of [[Consensus Is Not Evidence]]: both insist that something widely believed, including by you, still has to be tested against an outcome. It also guards against the failure mode in [[A Stale Source Is Confidently Wrong]], where fluency is mistaken for correctness.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Calibration cuts against the useful self-deception in [[Delusional and Disciplined]] — founders and artists often need a belief in the improbable that an honest probability estimate would kill in its first week. And a journal can curdle into scorekeeping, the anxious self-measurement [[Toxic Productivity]] warns about, where tracking replaces the thing being tracked.
