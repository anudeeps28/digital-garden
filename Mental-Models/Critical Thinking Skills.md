# Critical Thinking Skills

## Idea
Four specific cognitive skills — analysis, logical thinking, judgment, and questioning — determine whether AI makes you smarter or dumber.

## Definition
Stanford and the American Philosophical Association define critical thinking as "careful thinking directed to a goal." In the AI era, four skills within that framework matter most: **Analysis** — spotting what's missing or incorrect in a response. What assumptions were made? What variables were ignored? **Logical Thinking** — decomposing an answer into parts and adapting it to your specific context, not just copy-pasting. **Judgment** — making decisions with incomplete information. AI gives you data, frameworks, and perspectives, but it can't tell you what's right for *you*. **Questioning** — asking follow-ups instead of accepting the first answer. "What assumptions did you make?" "What's the strongest argument against this?" Most people accept the first response. These four skills are what separate using AI from being used by it.

## Source
Stanford's critical thinking framework and the American Philosophical Association's Delphi Report on critical thinking (1990, Peter Facione). Applied to AI usage by Anudeep Sharma (YouTube, August 2026), drawing on MIT's preliminary "Brain on LLM" study on cognitive effects of AI dependency.

## Compass

**Roots** — These skills are the operational layer of [[AI as Amplifier]] — they're the specific muscles that determine which side of the amplification curve you land on, and they connect to the classical tradition of Socratic questioning in [[Stoicism]].

**Paths** — Practicing these skills leads to [[The Exit Condition]] (judgment about what "done" means), [[Depth Is Cheap]] (analysis and questioning are what remain expensive), and better [[Prompt Engineering]] — because good prompts are just good questions.

**Neighbors** — This lives near [[Reading and Writing as Cognitive Gym]] (the daily exercises that keep these skills sharp), [[Selective Intelligence]] (knowing *when* to deploy critical thinking), and [[AI Is a Tool, Not Magic]] (understanding the tool is step one of thinking critically about its output).

**Clash** — Over-applying critical thinking to every AI interaction creates friction that defeats the purpose of using AI at all — [[The Exit Condition]] warns against infinite refinement loops, and sometimes "good enough" from autopilot mode is genuinely good enough for low-stakes tasks.
