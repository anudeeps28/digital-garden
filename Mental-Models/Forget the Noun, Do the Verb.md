# Forget the Noun, Do the Verb

## Idea
Chase the activity (writing, building, painting), not the title (writer, founder, artist) or its rewards, because the title can be lost and the activity is always available.

## Definition
People often want to be the noun: an author, a YouTuber, an entrepreneur. The noun brings status, but it also brings anxiety about whether you deserve it, and it ties your sense of self to outcomes you don't control. The verb is just the work: write today, ship today, film today. Kleon's advice is to focus on doing the verb and let the noun take care of itself, or never arrive, without that mattering. It also protects you when the title goes away, through rejection, a career change or a flop, because nothing stops you from doing the verb. Practically, measure your days by whether you did the thing, not by whether you've become someone.

## Source
Austin Kleon, *[[Keep Going]]* (2019), principle #3. It echoes the Stoic split between process and outcome, and the *Bhagavad Gita*'s teaching of acting without attachment to results.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It applies [[The Dichotomy of Control]] to creative identity: the verb is in your control, and the noun is mostly up to other people.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Doing the verb every day is [[Consistency]], and the verb is where [[Motivation Follows Action]]: you start working, then the motivation shows up. Held lightly, it becomes [[Full Effort, Loose Grip]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[There Is Nothing to Prove]] removes the pressure to earn the title. [[Tie Your Identity to How You Think]] makes a related career point: anchor yourself to a lasting ability, not a label that might be automated away.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
James Clear's *Atomic Habits* argues the reverse: identity-based habits ("I am a runner") make behavior stick. The two meet if the noun you adopt describes someone who does the verb ("I'm someone who writes daily") rather than a status you've earned ("I'm an author").
