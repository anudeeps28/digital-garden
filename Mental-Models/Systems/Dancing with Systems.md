# Dancing with Systems

## Idea
Complex systems cannot be controlled, only understood, influenced and redesigned in partnership, so the right stance is attentive responsiveness rather than command.

## Definition
Meadows ends the book by admitting that systems thinking does not give the control it seems to promise. You cannot predict or steer a complex system the way you engineer a bridge. You can learn its rhythm and move with it. Her guidelines: **Get the beat of the system**: watch how it actually behaves over time before you intervene. **Expose your mental models**: write them down and draw them so others can spot the flaws. **Honor information**: keep it accurate, timely and undistorted. **Use language with care**: avoid jargon and euphemism that blur what is happening. **Pay attention to what is important, not just what is quantifiable**: justice, resilience and quality are real even when you can't count them. **Build feedback policies for feedback systems**: rules that adjust to the state of the system. **Respect the system's own self-organization** instead of overriding local knowledge. **Locate responsibility**: make the people who decide also feel the consequences. **Stay humble and keep learning**: treat every intervention as an experiment.

## Source
Donella H. Meadows, *Thinking in Systems*, Chapter 7 ("Living in a World of Systems"), adapted from her essay "Dancing with Systems" (2001). See [[Thinking in Systems]].

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is what [[Systems Thinking]] concludes once you accept that [[Every Model Is a Simplification]]. If the map is always partial, the only reliable strategy is continuous observation and adjustment.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
In practice it means [[Instrument the Hypothesis]] before acting, keeping a [[Prediction Journal]] to check your models, and using [[Writing Is Thinking]] to expose them. "Locate responsibility" is the structural fix for the [[Tragedy of the Commons]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[The Dichotomy of Control]] and [[The Serenity Prayer]] share the humility: act on what you can influence and stop pretending to control the rest. [[Kaizen]] is the trial-and-error loop in practice: small experiments, honest feedback, adjust.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
"Dance with it" can become a reason to put off necessary decisive action, since some crises need a hard intervention, not a slow listen. And respecting self-organization is no help when the system's own goal is harmful, as in [[Seeking the Wrong Goal]].
