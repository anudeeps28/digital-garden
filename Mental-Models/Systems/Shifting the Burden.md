# Shifting the Burden

## Idea
A quick fix that relieves the symptom slowly weakens the system's own ability to solve the problem, so you need more of the fix over time. That dependence is addiction.

## Definition
Meadows calls this trap "shifting the burden to the intervenor", or addiction. A system has some ability to correct itself. An outside intervention relieves the symptom faster, so it gets used. Meanwhile the underlying ability goes unused and wastes away, and the system now needs the intervention just to stay where it was. Painkillers instead of treating the injury, caffeine instead of sleep, a hero engineer firefighting instead of the team fixing the root cause, subsidies that keep a failing industry alive: all have this shape. The tell is that the dose keeps growing while the underlying problem stays. Meadows' advice: avoid the intervention if you can, and if you need one, design it to strengthen the system's own ability and plan your withdrawal from the start.

## Source
Donella H. Meadows, *Thinking in Systems*, Chapter 5, trap #6 ("Shifting the Burden to the Intervenor: Addiction"). The archetype is also in Peter Senge's *The Fifth Discipline* (1990). Note: the video summary called it "the Inventor", but the book's term is "Intervenor". See [[Thinking in Systems]].

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is a [[Balancing Feedback Loop]] that has been outsourced. The external fix takes over the correcting job, and the internal capacity, a stock in [[Stocks and Flows]] terms, slowly drains away.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
The healthy alternative is to restore the system's own feedback and self-correction, which is a high rung on [[Leverage Points]] and the "build in feedback policies" advice in [[Dancing with Systems]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Train What Can't Be Handed Over]] guards against this with AI tools, where outsourcing a skill entirely can let your own ability wither. [[Discipline Over Motivation]] is the personal version: relying on motivation spikes as the fix weakens the habit structure underneath.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Not every dependency is addiction. Specialization and tools are dependence by design, and that is fine when the outside fix is reliable and the internal skill is not worth keeping. The trap is only when the fix hides a problem that keeps getting worse.
