# Matthew Effect

## Idea
Advantage compounds: those who already have attention, money or status find it easier to get more, so small early differences, even pure luck, grow into large gaps.

## Definition
The name comes from the Gospel of Matthew (25:29): "For to every one who has will more be given… but from him who has not, even what he has will be taken away." It describes any system where rewards are proportional to what you already have. A simple simulation shows how strong it is. Give everyone the same starting money and let them make fair coin-toss bets each round. With no difference in skill, early winners can bet more, survive streaks of losses, and pull away, so wealth ends up concentrated. Platforms do the same thing with attention: recommendation algorithms favor content that is already popular, cited papers get cited more, and top results get the clicks. Initial luck plus a reinforcing loop is enough to produce a lot of inequality, without anyone having to rig the outcome.

## Source
Sociologist Robert K. Merton coined the term in "The Matthew Effect in Science" (*Science*, 1968), about famous scientists getting disproportionate credit. Herbert Simon's and Derek de Solla Price's "cumulative advantage" models (1950s–70s) and Barabási–Albert preferential attachment (1999) formalize it. Meadows models the same structure as [[Success to the Successful]]. The coin-toss framing comes from yard-sale and kinetic wealth-exchange models in econophysics.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is a [[Reinforcing Feedback Loop]] where the reward is proportional to the current stock, the social form of the system trap [[Success to the Successful]].

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Run long enough, cumulative advantage produces a [[Power Law Distribution]] of wealth, followers and citations. In networks, [[Metcalfe's Law]] makes it even steeper.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[The Flywheel Concept]] is the Matthew Effect deliberately aimed at your own work. [[Volume Negates Luck]] is the practical counter: if early luck decides who compounds, take more shots so you get more draws.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Advantage is not destiny. Incumbents get complacent, markets shift, and balancing forces like competition, regulation and regression to the mean wear leads down. The effect is strong, but it can be interrupted.
