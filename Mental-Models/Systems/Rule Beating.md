# Rule Beating

## Idea
When rules are imposed rather than understood, people follow the letter of the rule while defeating its purpose.

## Definition
Rule beating is evasive action that technically complies. A department spends its whole budget in December so next year's allocation is not cut. Developers write tests that execute code without asserting anything to satisfy a coverage threshold. Companies move profits to low-tax jurisdictions. Meadows' example: a US law protecting endangered species on private land led some landowners to clear habitat before rare species could be found there. Rule beating is useful feedback. It shows that the rule conflicts with what the people it governs actually want. Meadows' fix is to redesign the rule, or explain its purpose well enough, that people put their creativity into meeting its spirit rather than getting around it.

## Source
Donella H. Meadows, *Thinking in Systems*, Chapter 5, trap #7. See [[Thinking in Systems]].

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is a quiet cousin of [[Policy Resistance]]: actors with different goals neutralize a rule through compliance rather than open opposition.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
When the rule is a metric, rule beating turns into [[Seeking the Wrong Goal]], where the system optimizes the number instead of the thing. The fix lives at the "rules" and "goals" rungs of [[Leverage Points]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Report the Outcome, Not the Metric]] is a habit against rule beating your own targets. In AI, reward hacking is the same behavior from an optimizer that satisfies the stated objective in a way nobody intended.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Not all loophole-finding is bad. Sometimes the rule is wrong, and beating it is how its flaws come to light. The useful response is to read the workaround as data about the rule, not just to add another rule.
