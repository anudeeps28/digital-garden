# Linear Minds in a Nonlinear World

## Idea
We expect twice the input to give twice the output, but most real systems curve, bend and flip, so linear forecasts fail exactly when it matters.

## Definition
A relationship is nonlinear when cause and effect are not proportional. One fertilizer application raises the yield, the second raises it less, and the tenth poisons the soil. A little traffic flows freely, and a little more causes gridlock. Nonlinearity also changes which loop is in charge: a system dominated by growth can suddenly be dominated by a limit, and its whole behavior changes. People default to straight-line extrapolation because it is easy, and it works fine near the present. The errors build up over longer horizons and near thresholds. That is why models fitted to recent flows predict next quarter well and long-term structural change badly. Expect curves, especially diminishing returns, rising costs, and tipping points.

## Source
Donella H. Meadows, *Thinking in Systems*, Chapter 4 ("Why Systems Surprise Us"). The summary this note draws on adds an economist's critique: econometrics models flows and largely ignores the stocks (physical and human capital) that drive long-run change. See [[Thinking in Systems]].

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is one of the reasons systems surprise us, alongside [[Bounded Rationality]] and the fact that [[Every Model Is a Simplification]].

**Paths** — *where this leads*
It explains why [[Reinforcing Feedback Loop|reinforcing loops]] get ignored until it is too late, and why [[Limits to Growth]] arrive faster than expected: the last doubling looks small right up until it does not.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Percentile-Based-Performance-Metrics]] exist because latency is nonlinear: averages hide the tail where a queue tips into collapse. The [[Pareto Principle]] is another reminder that effort and outcome are not proportional.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Linear approximations are often good enough locally, and assuming nonlinearity everywhere can become an excuse to never forecast. The skill is knowing how far from the present the straight line still holds.
