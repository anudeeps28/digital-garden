# Seeking the Wrong Goal

## Idea
A system will efficiently produce whatever it is told to measure, so if the measure is not the real goal, you get the measure and not the goal.

## Definition
Meadows calls this the most insidious trap because the system is not failing. It is succeeding at the wrong thing. Her main example is GDP. It counts car crashes, medical bills and pollution cleanup as growth, and it ignores unpaid care work, leisure and the health of the environment, so a country can grow its GDP while its welfare declines. Smaller examples: schools that teach to the test, support teams that close tickets fast without solving them, engineering teams praised for lines of code. The danger is greatest when the metric is easy to count and the real goal is not. Her fix is to state the real goal precisely and to measure as close to it as possible, while remembering that some important things cannot be quantified.

## Source
Donella H. Meadows, *Thinking in Systems*, Chapter 5, trap #8. Closely related to Goodhart's Law (economist Charles Goodhart, 1975; usually quoted in Marilyn Strathern's 1997 phrasing, "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"), and to Campbell's Law (1976). See [[Thinking in Systems]].

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is what happens when [[The Purpose of a System Is What It Does]] drifts away from what it was meant to do: the metric quietly becomes the purpose.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
The fix is the "goals" rung of [[Leverage Points]], plus Meadows' advice in [[Dancing with Systems]] to pay attention to what is important, not just what is quantifiable. [[Goodhart's Law]] is the short form worth keeping as its own note.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Report the Outcome, Not the Metric]] is the direct practical counterpart. [[Rule Beating]] is how individuals game a bad goal, and this note is about the system pursuing one.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Without some metric, organizations drift toward vagueness and no one can tell progress from activity. The answer is not to drop measurement but to keep metrics subordinate to the goal and to retire them once they are being gamed.
