# Reinforcing Feedback Loop

## Idea
A reinforcing loop feeds on itself: more produces more and less produces less, so it creates exponential growth or collapse.

## Definition
In a reinforcing (positive) feedback loop, a stock's level increases its own inflow. Money earns interest, which adds to the money. Users attract more users. An insult provokes a bigger insult. "Positive" describes the direction of the effect, not whether it is good. The same loop that compounds savings also compounds debt, panic or an argument. Reinforcing loops produce exponential curves, and those feel slow for a long time and then suddenly very fast. No reinforcing loop runs forever, though. Eventually it hits a limit and a balancing loop takes over.

## Source
Donella H. Meadows, *Thinking in Systems*, Chapters 1–2. The exponential growth argument is the core of Meadows et al., *The Limits to Growth* (1972). See [[Thinking in Systems]].

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the other kind of feedback in [[Systems Thinking]], the engine of growth, set against the stabilizing [[Balancing Feedback Loop]].

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Left unchecked, it produces [[Limits to Growth]] and overshoot. Between people it produces [[Escalation]], and in competition it produces [[Success to the Successful]] and the [[Matthew Effect]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[The Flywheel Concept]] is a reinforcing loop you build on purpose: early effort is slow, then momentum feeds itself. [[Consistency Over Intensity]] and [[Kaizen]] rely on the same compounding. [[Thundering Herd]] is a failure version, where retries add load, which causes more failures and more retries.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Our intuition is linear, so we underestimate reinforcing loops early and panic about them late ([[Linear Minds in a Nonlinear World]]). Growth also always runs into a [[Limiting Factor]], so "it will keep compounding" is never true forever.
