# Bounded Rationality

## Idea
People make sensible decisions based on what they can see from where they stand, and those locally sensible choices can add up to outcomes nobody wanted.

## Definition
No one has complete information, unlimited time, or perfect foresight. People decide based on the slice of the system visible to them, on feedback that is late or missing, and on short-term incentives. A fisherman adding one more boat, a manager hitting this quarter's target, and a commuter choosing the fastest route are each rational within their bounds. Together they can deplete the fishery, hollow out the company, and jam the road. Meadows' conclusion is that swapping in "better" people rarely helps, because they inherit the same bounded view. You fix it by changing the system so that people at each point get better, more timely information and incentives that reflect the whole.

## Source
Herbert A. Simon coined "bounded rationality" in the 1950s (*Models of Man*, 1957) and won the 1978 Nobel Prize in economics partly for it. Meadows applies it in *Thinking in Systems*, Chapter 4. See [[Thinking in Systems]].

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the human reason systems surprise us, alongside [[Linear Minds in a Nonlinear World]], and it is why [[Systems Thinking]] focuses on structure over blame.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Bounded rationality with a shared resource produces the [[Tragedy of the Commons]]. Bounded rationality with clashing goals produces [[Policy Resistance]]. In both cases the remedy runs through [[Information Flows Shape Behavior]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[A Stale Source Is Confidently Wrong]] is bounded rationality caused by outdated information: correct reasoning from a premise that is no longer true. [[Environment Design]] accepts that you are bounded too and arranges your surroundings so the easy choice is the good one.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
It can excuse anything ("everyone was acting rationally"). Some actors do see the whole system and exploit it anyway, which is [[Rule Beating]] rather than limited vision.
