# Leverage Points

## Idea
Places to intervene in a system can be ranked by how much they change it. The levers people reach for first, numbers and parameters, are the weakest, and the strongest are goals and paradigms.

## Definition
Meadows ranks twelve intervention points. Grouped from weakest to strongest: **Numbers** (tax rates, budgets, thresholds) are easy to argue about and rarely change behavior. **Physical structure** (stocks, flows, infrastructure) matters but is slow and expensive to change. **Delays and buffers** set whether the system is stable or oscillates. **Feedback loops** decide whether it corrects itself or runs away: protect the balancing loops (antitrust, whistle-blower protection) and slow the reinforcing ones. **Information flows** decide who sees what and when. **Rules** set incentives and constraints, and power lies with whoever writes them. **Self-organization** is the system's ability to change its own structure. **Goals** are what the whole thing is trying to do. **Paradigms** are the shared assumptions from which goals and rules arise. Above even these sits the ability to step outside any paradigm. Her counterintuitive note: people often find the lever and then push it in the wrong direction.

## Source
Donella H. Meadows, "Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System" (Sustainability Institute, 1999), reproduced as Chapter 6 of *Thinking in Systems*. The video summary grouped her twelve points into eight. See [[Thinking in Systems]].

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It turns [[Systems Thinking]] into a strategy question, and its ranking mirrors [[Elements, Interconnections, and Purpose]]: parts at the bottom, purpose near the top.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Each system trap has its exit on a specific rung: [[Seeking the Wrong Goal]] at goals, [[Tragedy of the Commons]] at rules and information, and [[Escalation]] at paradigms. The practical approach to working these levers is [[Dancing with Systems]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
The [[Pareto Principle]] and [[Limiting Factor]] also say effort is unevenly rewarded, so find the few places that move everything. [[Environment Design]] is a personal leverage move: change structure and defaults instead of willpower, which is the numbers rung.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
The high-leverage points are also the hardest to reach and the most resisted. Paradigms do not shift on command, and most people control mainly numbers. Leverage is not a ranking of what is easy, and Meadows admits the list itself is "tentative".
