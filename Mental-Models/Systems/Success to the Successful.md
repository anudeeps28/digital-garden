# Success to the Successful

## Idea
When winners get resources that help them win again, an early lead compounds into dominance and everyone else is pushed out.

## Definition
In this trap, winning brings rewards, and those rewards are exactly the means to win the next round. The richer player in Monopoly buys more hotels, collects more rent, and buys still more. A startup with slightly more users raises more money and outspends competitors. A well-funded school attracts better teachers, gets better results, and gets more funding. The outcome is competitive exclusion: one player takes nearly everything, often not because they were much better but because they got ahead first. Meadows' fixes are ways to level the field: diversification (losers can go play a different game), antitrust limits on the winner's share, and policies that give losers a fair shot again, such as progressive taxation or inheritance limits.

## Source
Donella H. Meadows, *Thinking in Systems*, Chapter 5, trap #5. It matches the "competitive exclusion principle" in ecology (G. F. Gause, 1934) and the archetype of the same name in Peter Senge's *The Fifth Discipline* (1990). See [[Thinking in Systems]].

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is a [[Reinforcing Feedback Loop]] in which each competitor's success feeds that competitor's next round, while the losers' supply is cut off.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
It produces the [[Matthew Effect]] and the skewed outcomes of a [[Power Law Distribution]]. In networked products, [[Metcalfe's Law]] speeds it up into winner-take-all markets.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[The Flywheel Concept]] is the same loop seen from the winner's side. [[Volume Negates Luck]] is the personal response: you cannot control the early dice, but more attempts give you more chances to land an early win that compounds.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Not every lead is unfair. Some winners really are better, and leveling too hard punishes real merit. Meadows' answer is not to equalize outcomes but to keep the game open, so a better newcomer can still get in.
