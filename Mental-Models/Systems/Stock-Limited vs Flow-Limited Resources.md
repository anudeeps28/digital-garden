# Stock-Limited vs Flow-Limited Resources

## Idea
A non-renewable resource is limited by how much of it exists. A renewable resource is limited by how fast it can regenerate, and overusing it can destroy that regeneration.

## Definition
Oil in the ground is **stock-limited**: there is a fixed amount, and the faster you extract it the sooner it runs out. Usually the decline comes from rising extraction costs rather than the stock hitting zero. Fish, forests and groundwater are **flow-limited**: you can harvest indefinitely as long as you take no more than the regeneration rate. Overharvest a renewable resource and it does not just shrink, its capacity to regenerate can collapse, which turns a renewable resource into a non-renewable one. Personal resources follow the same rule. Attention, sleep and goodwill are renewable within a rate. Spend past that rate for long enough and you damage the regeneration itself, which is what burnout is.

## Source
Donella H. Meadows, *Thinking in Systems*, Chapter 2 (systems-zoo models of a non-renewable oil economy and a renewable fishing economy). Related to the "maximum sustainable yield" concept in fisheries science. See [[Thinking in Systems]].

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It applies [[Stocks and Flows]] to [[Limits to Growth]]: the type of limit decides whether the risk is running out or wearing out.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Flow-limited shared resources are where the [[Tragedy of the Commons]] happens: each user overharvests a little and the shared regeneration rate collapses.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
The [[85% Rule]] and [[Slow Productivity]] treat personal energy as flow-limited: sustain a pace below the regeneration rate instead of running down the stock. [[Rate Limiting]] protects a service's capacity in the same way.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
In practice the regeneration rate is uncertain and changes over time, so a "sustainable yield" set confidently can still be overharvest. That is why [[Every Model Is a Simplification]] is the warning that should come with any sustainability target.
