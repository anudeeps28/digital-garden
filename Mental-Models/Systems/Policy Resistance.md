# Policy Resistance

## Idea
When several actors pull a system toward different goals, any push from one side is met by stronger pushes from the others, so the system barely moves however hard anyone tries.

## Definition
Policy resistance (Meadows calls it "fixes that fail") happens when each actor has their own balancing loop aimed at a different target. A government restricts a drug, the price rises, suppliers are drawn in, and enforcement pushes harder. Every side escalates its effort and the outcome stays about where it was, only at a higher cost. Romania's 1967 ban on abortion and contraception is her starkest example. Births spiked and then fell back as people found workarounds, with tragic side effects. The way out is not a bigger push. Either let go and let the opposing efforts relax, or find a goal that all the actors share so their loops stop fighting.

## Source
Donella H. Meadows, *Thinking in Systems*, Chapter 5 ("System Traps… and Opportunities"), trap #1. See [[Thinking in Systems]].

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is several [[Balancing Feedback Loop|balancing loops]] tugging against each other, and each actor is acting reasonably within their [[Bounded Rationality]].

**Paths** — *where this leads*
The exit Meadows recommends is changing the goal. That is the "goals" rung of [[Leverage Points]], and it often requires a shared paradigm rather than a stronger rule.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Rule Beating]] is a quieter form of resistance, where people comply on paper but not in spirit. [[Escalation]] has the same arms-race shape, except both sides want more than each other rather than different things.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
"Just let go" is not always available. Some resisted policies, like safety rules, protect people who have no seat at the table. In those cases the job is to lower the cost of complying rather than to give up.
