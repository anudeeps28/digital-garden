# The Four Pillars of Happiness

## Idea
Happiness rests on four pillars — faith, family, friendship, and work — and neglecting any one of them makes the structure unstable.

## Definition
Arthur Brooks' framework identifies four irreducible domains that sustain a fulfilling life. *Faith* (or a life philosophy) provides transcendence and humility — a perspective larger than yourself. *Family* creates irreplaceable, mystical bonds that no other relationship can substitute. *Friendship* offers deep, non-transactional love — connection without agenda. *Work* is where you earn success and serve others, giving structure and purpose. The insight is that these aren't interchangeable: crushing it at work can't compensate for absent friendships, and a rich social life can't fill the void if you have no sense of meaning. Neglect one and happiness starts to fade; nurture all four and life becomes rich.

## Source
Arthur Brooks, social scientist, Harvard professor, and author of *From Strength to Strength* (2022); shared via Instagram @arthurcbrooks.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This framework belongs to the deeper inquiry into [[Happiness]] — what actually makes a life feel good, as opposed to what we think should make it feel good. It pushes back against single-variable theories (just find your passion, just make enough money) by insisting on a portfolio model.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
The four pillars challenge [[The Deferred Life Plan]] directly — if you defer family and friendship until you "make it" at work, you're structurally undermining your own happiness. It also connects to [[Hedonic Adaptation]], which explains why success in one pillar alone never feels like enough.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Stoicism]] and [[Amor Fati]] provide the philosophical grounding for the faith/life philosophy pillar — a framework for accepting what you cannot control. [[Mental Clarity and Meaning]] neighbors this as the practice of actually auditing which pillars you're neglecting.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
[[Toxic Productivity]] is the most common way people sacrifice the other three pillars for work. The hard truth in Brooks' framework is that the pillar most people over-index on (work) is the one with the steepest diminishing returns.
