# Learning Agility

## Idea
The ability to learn new things fast — regardless of domain — is the single most durable career skill, because every other skill eventually expires.

## Definition
Learning agility is the meta-skill that makes all other skills acquirable. It's not intelligence or talent — it's the practiced ability to enter an unfamiliar domain, map its landscape, build functional competence quickly, and know when you've learned enough to act. In a world where AI commoditizes specialized depth and technical skills become obsolete in years rather than decades, the person who can be dropped into an unfamiliar problem and figure it out anyway is the most valuable person in the room. Companies describe their best people not as the most specialized, but as the "Swiss army knife" — flexible, fast-learning, comfortable with discomfort. Learning agility compounds: each new domain you learn makes the next one faster, because you start recognizing meta-patterns across fields.

## Source
The term appears in leadership development research by Michael Lombardo and Robert Eichinger at the Center for Creative Leadership (2000), who identified it as the strongest predictor of executive success. Applied to the AI era by Anudeep Sharma (YouTube, August 2026), where the argument is that learning agility matters more than any specific major, framework, or technical skill.

## Compass

**Roots** — This is the hub that connects [[The IDE Method]] (the system for learning fast), [[Familiar, Not Easy]] (the reframe that makes unfamiliarity a solvable problem), and [[We Learn by Doing, Not by Watching]] (the mechanism by which agility is built — reps, not consumption).

**Paths** — Learning agility leads directly to [[Depth Is Cheap]] — if depth can be borrowed through AI, the person who learns fastest captures the most value — and to [[The Paradox of Freedom]], because the ability to learn anything is the ultimate form of professional freedom.

**Neighbors** — This lives near [[Managing Ambiguity]] (comfort with not-knowing is a prerequisite for learning agility), [[85% Rule]] (calibrating the difficulty of what you're learning for optimal growth), and [[Kaizen]] (building learning agility through consistent daily practice).

**Clash** — [[Consistency]] and [[Consistency Over Intensity]] push back on the "learn anything fast" framing — real mastery in any single domain takes years, and learning agility without depth produces a "mile wide, inch deep" generalist who can start everything but finish nothing.
