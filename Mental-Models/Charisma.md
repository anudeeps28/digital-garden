# Charisma

## Idea
Charisma isn't an innate gift — it's a learnable combination of warmth and competence that makes people feel both valued and impressed.

## Definition
Charisma breaks down into two components: warmth (the sense that you care about others) and competence (the sense that you can deliver on what you promise). Warmth signals trustworthiness; competence signals capability. People instinctively evaluate both, and the combination is what makes someone magnetic. You can be brilliant but cold (respected, not followed), or warm but incompetent (liked, not trusted). Charisma is the rare overlap where both signals fire simultaneously. It gets you remembered in rooms — and that memory is a form of leverage.

## Source
Social psychology research by Susan Fiske and Amy Cuddy on the warmth-competence model; popularized in Olivia Fox Cabane's *The Charisma Myth* (2012).

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
Charisma sits at the intersection of social psychology and leadership, rooted in the warmth-competence model that governs how humans evaluate each other in milliseconds. The deeper question it belongs to is what makes someone influential — and why some people command rooms while others with the same credentials don't.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
If charisma is learnable, it becomes a form of leverage — an unfair advantage you can deliberately build. This connects directly to [[Networking]] and [[Show Your Work]], where being memorable and trustworthy compounds your surface area for opportunity.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
Charisma lives near [[You Cannot Compete with Someone Who Is Having Fun]] — authentic energy is a form of warmth that draws people in without effort. It also neighbors the concept of executive presence, which is essentially charisma applied in a corporate context.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
The risk is performing charisma without substance — all warmth, no competence, which eventually collapses into manipulation. [[There Is Nothing to Prove]] pushes against the temptation to optimize for impression rather than genuine connection.
