# Taste Is Earned Conviction

## Idea
Taste is knowing what good looks like without being told, and it is a residue of having made a ridiculous quantity of things — which is why it stays scarce as making gets cheap.

## Definition
Taste is not knowing right from wrong; textbooks cover that. It is being able to look at something and see three moves ahead — this will cause problems, this won't, this is what people will actually like, this will fall apart when ten thousand users hit it. You cannot get it from a textbook because it is compressed experience: years of slop, then weeks of slop, then a little less sloppy, and eventually you just know. The reason it matters more now is that generation has become free. When anyone can produce a working app in an afternoon, producing is no longer the bottleneck and discriminating is. A product built by someone with taste announces itself immediately; so does one where every decision was made because a model suggested it.

## Source
Captured from my own reel on taste in software engineering (September 2026) — "taste is just conviction that's been earned."

Paul Graham made the case that taste is a real, learnable faculty rather than mere subjective preference in "Taste for Makers" (2002), ending on "the recipe for great work is: very exacting taste, plus the ability to gratify it." Ira Glass supplies the other half — the beginner's gap, where your taste is already good enough to see that your work isn't, and the only way through is volume. The empirical version is the pottery-class experiment from Bayles and Orland's *Art & Fear*, where the group graded on quantity produced the best work.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
Its evidence base is [[The Parable of the Pottery Class]] — quantity of practice produces quality, and taste is what quantity leaves behind. It depends on the arithmetic in [[Volume Negates Luck]], since the reps have to be numerous enough for the pattern-recognition to form at all.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Taste is the clearest example of a capacity you cannot delegate, which makes it the sharp end of [[Train What Can't Be Handed Over]], and it is the substrate that stops [[Tie Your Identity to How You Think]] from collapsing into generic cleverness. In a market where [[Depth Is Cheap]], it is close to the only thing left that isn't.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It is built the same way as the competence in [[We Learn by Doing, Not by Watching]], and it explains the mechanism behind [[Familiar, Not Easy]] — what looks like an easy judgment to the principal engineer is simply a familiar one, seen a few hundred times before.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
[[AI Removes the Barriers]] pushes back hard: if effort is now the only barrier, then the long apprenticeship taste requires may be a toll nobody has to pay any more, and years of slop may just be years lost. Taste also calcifies — the practitioner who has seen it all is the one most likely to reject the thing that works for reasons the old reps can't explain, which is exactly the failure in [[Consensus Is Not Evidence]].
