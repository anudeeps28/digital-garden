# The Material Talks Back

## Idea
What you make never matches what was in your head, because the idea only finishes forming once it meets the real material, so start before you are ready and treat the first version as the first of five.

## Definition
Every note, script and app comes out different from what you pictured the moment you start making it. However thorough the PRD and design documents, once the thing takes shape in front of you, you see what you didn't think of and what you got wrong. That isn't a failure of planning. It is how making works: the material, whether words, code or clay, answers back and shows you what the idea actually was. Planning still matters, but it can't replace the conversation. So be willing to make a bad first version. The first try is really the first draft of the fifth version you'll end up with, and you only reach the fifth by making the first four.

## Source
Captured from my own reel "Be willing to look bad" (October 2026).

The concept is Donald Schön's, from *The Reflective Practitioner* (1983). His chapter "Design as a Reflective Conversation with the Situation" describes practitioners making a move, the situation "talking back", and the designer reframing in response. Fred Brooks's "plan to throw one away; you will, anyhow" (*The Mythical Man-Month*, 1975) is the software version, and Ira Glass's "the gap" between taste and ability (2009) is the emotional one.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It explains why [[The Parable of the Pottery Class]] works: the quantity group got more rounds of the clay talking back. It is also the creative version of [[Writing Is Thinking]], since you find out what you meant by trying to say it.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
It argues for [[First Make It Work, Then Make It Better]] and [[A Draft, Not a Verdict]], and for [[Approve the Foundation Before Polish]], because the first version's job is to talk back, not to be finished.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits near [[Motivation Follows Action]], since starting before you are ready is how you get the material to answer, and [[Be the Worst in the Room]], since both ask you to accept looking bad in exchange for learning.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Some material doesn't forgive. A bad first version of a bridge, a database migration or a one-way decision can't just be thrown away, and [[One-Way and Two-Way Doors]] marks where you should plan first and build second.
