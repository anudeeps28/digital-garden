# Consensus Is Not Evidence

## Idea
Most "rules" you follow were never proven — they were just agreed upon by people who never tried breaking them.

## Definition
The tendency to treat social consensus as evidence that something is true or necessary, when in reality no one tested the assumption. "That's not how it's done" feels like proof, but it's just agreement — inherited from people who never questioned it. The moment you stop treating consensus as truth, you stop waiting for permission that was never coming.

## Source
Anudeep Sharma's content (Instagram, July 2026). The idea draws on a long tradition of contrarian thinking — from Galileo to Steve Jobs' "Think Different" — but the specific framing of "says who?" as the only question that matters is the note's core.

## Compass

**Roots** — This grows out of [[The Dichotomy of Control]] and Stoic philosophy — the recognition that most external "rules" are opinions, not facts, and that your locus of control starts with questioning inherited assumptions.

**Paths** — Once you internalize this, it unlocks [[Delusional and Disciplined]] energy — the willingness to pursue goals that look unrealistic to consensus-followers, and connects to the entrepreneurial mindset in [[You Don't Need Money to Make Money]].

**Neighbors** — This sits beside [[There Is Nothing to Prove]], which shares the same independence from external validation, and [[Full Effort, Loose Grip]], which also requires acting without guaranteed permission or outcomes.

**Clash** — This pushes against the safety of [[Managing Ambiguity]] — sometimes consensus exists because the collective has learned something genuinely useful, and breaking rules without understanding why they exist is just recklessness dressed as courage.
