# Zero-Sum vs Positive-Sum Games

## Idea
The most miserable people only play zero-sum games; the happiest have at least one pursuit where nobody has to lose for them to win.

## Definition
A zero-sum game is one where your gain requires someone else's loss — class rank, corporate promotions, competitive sports brackets. A positive-sum game is one where the pie grows — fitness, content creation, open source, entrepreneurship. Communities built around zero-sum games tend toward toxicity because every interaction is subtly adversarial, while positive-sum communities are generous because your success doesn't threaten mine — it may even help. You can't avoid zero-sum games entirely, but if every pursuit in your life is zero-sum, life becomes an endless cycle of taking and being taken from.

## Source
Anudeep Sharma's content (Instagram, July 2026). The zero-sum concept originates from John von Neumann and Oskar Morgenstern's "Theory of Games and Economic Behavior" (1944). The positive-sum framing is widely used in startup and creator culture, notably by Naval Ravikant.
