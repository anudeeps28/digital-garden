# Rank, Don't Filter

## Idea
Use scores to sort options, not to throw them away. Keep only a few true knockout rules as hard gates, and let everything else compete on rank.

## Definition
A score feels objective, so it is tempting to set a threshold and discard everything below it. But most scores are noisy blends of soft signals, and a cutoff turns that noise into a permanent decision you never see again. **Ranking** keeps the information: low-scoring options sink to the bottom but remain visible, and a person can still pull one up. The discipline is to separate the two kinds of criteria. A handful are genuine **knockouts** (wrong country, required licence missing, salary below a floor you won't accept) and those filter. Everything else ranks. A worked example from a job search pipeline: a role scored as a 55% fit stays in the inbox, tagged *stretch*, instead of vanishing. The limit is applied at the other end, on the action stage: tailor only 10 to 20 applications a week, because spraying volume tends to be punished by the very systems you're trying to reach.

## Source
Decision research calls this the difference between **non-compensatory** rules (conjunctive screening, where failing any cutoff eliminates an option) and **compensatory** rules (weighted scoring, where strengths offset weaknesses). Consumer-choice studies typically model real decisions as a two-stage process: a light screen to form a consideration set, then compensatory ranking within it. The specific framing here is a synthesis.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It rests on the same humility as [[Confidence Score]]: a number expresses how likely, not whether, and treating it as a verdict throws away its uncertainty.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Ranking lets the [[Pareto Principle]] do its work at the top of the list while keeping the long tail recoverable, and pairs well with [[Semantic Re-ranking]] in search systems that face the same choice.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Selective Intelligence]] is about choosing where attention goes, and ranking is the mechanism that makes that choice reversible. [[The Eisenhower Matrix]] is a ranking with exactly two knockout axes.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
[[Volume Negates Luck]] argues for sending more, not fewer, and in some games the count genuinely matters. The tension resolves only by knowing whether your channel rewards persistence or punishes spam.
