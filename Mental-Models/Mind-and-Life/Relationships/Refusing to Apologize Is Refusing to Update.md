# Refusing to Apologize Is Refusing to Update

## Idea
Saying sorry takes four seconds; refusing to say it is a refusal to change your mind in public, which is the opposite of intelligence.

## Definition
An apology is a belief update said out loud: "I was wrong, and I've changed my view." The smartest people change their minds the moment they're shown they're wrong, so refusing to apologise isn't a sign of strength. It's a refusal to update, made worse by a social cost: not saying sorry can cost you a friendship that four seconds would have saved. It belongs to a family of signs that someone is not seeing what is in front of them. **Belittling a friend's win** treats success as a zero-sum trophy. **Mocking beginners** usually comes from someone who stopped trying new things. **Obsessing over one person** means you are relating to a story you built about them rather than to them. In each case, intelligence isn't the degree. It is whether you keep checking your picture against reality.

## Source
Captured from my own reel on things that make me question someone's intelligence (October 2026).

The research explains why refusing feels good even when it costs you. Okimoto, Wenzel & Hedrick (2013, *European Journal of Social Psychology*) found that people who refused to apologise for a wrong had *higher* self-esteem afterwards, through a greater sense of power, control and integrity. The ego pays for the refusal. On the updating side, there is Paul Saffo's "strong opinions, weakly held" (2008) and the Bayesian idea of revising beliefs in proportion to new evidence, which Philip Tetlock's *Superforecasting* (2015) found in the best forecasters.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It applies [[Critical Thinking Skills]] to relationships: an apology is what updating looks like when the evidence is someone you hurt. [[Errare Humanum Est]] is the premise, since being wrong is normal and staying wrong is the choice.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
The same habit improves predictions, which is what a [[Prediction Journal]] trains: writing down when you were wrong is a private apology to reality. The "belittling a win" sign is a failure to see [[Zero-Sum vs Positive-Sum Games]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits beside [[Cruelty Is Not Honesty]], another sign from the same list, and [[Consensus Is Not Evidence]], which describes the same refusal at group scale: holding a view because changing it costs status.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Not every apology is an update. Reflexive "sorry" can be appeasement, and apologising for something you still believe is dishonest. The Okimoto finding is a reminder that refusing has a real psychological payoff, so "just apologise" underestimates what it costs the ego.
