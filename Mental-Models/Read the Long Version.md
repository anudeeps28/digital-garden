# Read the Long Version

## Idea
Let AI take the shortcut on the work, but keep the long version for the learning, because a summary hands you the answer while understanding lives in the details.

## Definition
Everything is getting shrunk: the article, the video, the caption a friend sends you. A summary gives you the conclusion, but understanding comes from holding the whole thing in your head at once and making sense of how the pieces fit, and that only happens if you went through the pieces. Constant summarising also wears down patience, and patience is a skill. The habit has two parts. **Read full things:** the whole post, start to end, even at 15 or 20 minutes. **Learn with AI first, then go long:** use AI to build first-principles basics with flashcards, question banks and plain-language explanations, and then watch the full lecture. A long seminar hits differently when you already have the scaffolding, because now you can catch the depth.

## Source
Captured from two of my own reels on anti-AI-brainrot habits (October 2026).

The "learn the basics first, then go long" sequence matches *schema theory* and David Ausubel's *advance organisers* (1960): prior structure makes new material easier to absorb. The worry about summaries is close to the "cognitive debt" finding of Kosmyna et al. ("Your Brain on ChatGPT", MIT Media Lab, 2025; small sample, not peer reviewed). Nicholas Carr's *The Shallows* (2010) made the earlier version of this argument about the web.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the reading-side version of [[Reading and Writing as Cognitive Gym]]: once machines remove the effort, the effort has to be chosen on purpose. It belongs to [[Train What Can't Be Handed Over]], because patience and depth cannot be delegated.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Long reading feeds [[Deep Work]] and [[Writing Is Thinking]], since you can only write well about what you have actually held in your head. It pairs with [[Think on Paper First]] as the input half of the same habit.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits beside [[Hand Off the Code, Not the Understanding]], the same rule for building, and [[Only the Rich Will Burn Candles]], which predicts that the long version becomes a luxury once summaries become the default.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Not everything deserves the long version. Most content is filler, and [[Selective Intelligence]] and the [[Pareto Principle]] argue for summarising the many so you can go deep on the few. The skill is choosing which is which, not reading everything in full.
