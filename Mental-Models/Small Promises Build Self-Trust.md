# Small Promises Build Self-Trust

## Idea
Every promise you keep to yourself makes the next one easier to believe, so keep the promises small enough that you actually keep them.

## Definition
Self-trust works like trust in anyone else: it is built from a record of commitments kept. Promise yourself a two-hour workout and skip it, and you have taught yourself that your own word is optional. Promise ten minutes and do it, and you have one more piece of evidence that when you say you'll do something, you do. That record matters more than motivation, because on low days it is what you lean on: "I said I would, and I usually do." Two practices support it. **Track your wins**, so that on days when nothing feels like progress you have proof that it is. **Shrink the promise** until keeping it is almost certain, then let the streak do the work.

## Source
Captured from my own reel on ways to stop relying on motivation (October 2026).

The psychology is Albert Bandura's self-efficacy theory ("Self-efficacy: Toward a Unifying Theory of Behavioral Change", 1977). Of its four sources of belief in your own ability, *performance accomplishments*, later called mastery experiences, are the strongest. The "keep promises to yourself" framing has no single originator. Its best-documented source is Stephen M.R. Covey's *The Speed of Trust* (2006), where self-trust comes from making and keeping commitments to yourself. Teresa Amabile and Steven Kramer's *The Progress Principle* (2011) backs the "track your wins" half: small visible progress is the strongest daily motivator at work.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is one of the mechanisms behind [[Discipline Over Motivation]]: discipline lasts because it is backed by evidence about yourself, not by feeling. It pairs with [[Motivation Follows Action]], which shrinks the start for the same reason this shrinks the promise.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Kept promises compound into identity, the [[Consistency Over Intensity]] effect, and a streak is a small [[Reinforcing Feedback Loop]]: each kept promise makes the next one more likely. [[The Daily Highlight]] is one way to set a single small promise each day.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits near [[Kaizen]], which makes improvement so small it can't fail, and [[Environment Design]], which removes the decision so the promise is easier to keep.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Promises that are too small can become a way of hiding from real goals, and a broken streak can feel like a total failure and lead to quitting. [[Errare Humanum Est]] is the counterweight: the record is about the pattern, not perfection.
