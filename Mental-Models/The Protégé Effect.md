# The Protégé Effect

## Idea
Preparing to teach something, and then teaching it, exposes the holes in your own understanding faster than any amount of further study.

## Definition
Studying lets you stay in recognition — the material looks familiar, so you assume you know it. Teaching forces production: you have to choose an order, pick what to leave out, and answer a question you did not script. The holes surface immediately, and they surface as specific gaps rather than a vague sense of being shaky. The useful consequence is that you do not need to wait until you have mastered something to teach it. Teaching early is the fastest way to find out what you actually have, which makes it a diagnostic rather than a reward for expertise.

## Source
Captured from my own reel on habits for the AI age (September 2026) — "teach someone what you just learned, and you find the holes in your learning immediately."

The effect is named in the learning-science literature. The most-cited demonstration is Catherine Chase, Doris Chin, Marily Oppezzo and Daniel Schwartz's 2009 teachable-agents study, where middle-school students who studied in order to teach a digital character worked harder, revised more and scored better on the hardest questions than students studying for themselves. Rod Roscoe and Michelene Chi's work on tutor learning distinguishes the mechanism: gains come from knowledge-*building* — generating explanations and fielding questions — rather than knowledge-*telling*. The practice far predates the name, from Jean-Pol Martin's *Lernen durch Lehren* to the technique popularly attached to Richard Feynman.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This is a specific instance of the bias toward production over consumption in [[We Learn by Doing, Not by Watching]] — teaching is doing, where re-reading is watching. It is one of the undelegatable capacities listed under [[Train What Can't Be Handed Over]], since the model can explain the topic but cannot discover your gaps for you.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Taken seriously it changes how you write for others: [[Explain in Layers]] is the structural answer to having to teach the same idea to people at different depths, and [[Show Your Work]] turns the teaching into a public artifact that compounds.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It is the spoken twin of [[Writing Is Thinking]] — both claim that the act of externalising is where the understanding is actually formed, not merely transmitted. It shares its mechanism with [[Reading and Writing as Cognitive Gym]], where the effort is the point.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Teaching before you understand also broadcasts your errors with the fluency of a lesson, which is exactly the failure in [[A Stale Source Is Confidently Wrong]] — a confident wrong explanation gives the listener no signal that anything is off. And explaining too early can harden a shallow model into a story you now have a stake in defending, which is the opposite of [[A Draft, Not a Verdict]].
