# Approve the Foundation Before Polish

## Idea
Get the rough structure signed off before adding any finish, because polish applied to a wrong foundation is thrown away when the foundation changes.

## Definition
Every piece of work has a layer that everything else sits on: the order of shots in a video, the outline of an essay, the data model of an app. Polish (captions, effects, colour, copy edits, animations) is expensive and fragile because it is tied to that layer. Move a scene and its captions, transitions, and music cues all need redoing. **Approving the foundation first** means stopping at the rough version, showing it to whoever decides, and getting an explicit yes before spending a minute on finish. A worked example: in an AI-assisted video workflow, the rough trim is reviewed and approved before any captions or effects are generated. Rejected cuts at that stage cost seconds; rejected cuts after captioning cost the whole captioning pass.

## Source
Film post-production's **picture lock**: the point where the edit's shot order and timing are frozen so that sound, colour grading, and visual effects teams can work without their effort being undone. The software analogue is Kent Beck's "make it work, make it right, make it fast", which orders the same concern.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is a specific case of [[First Make It Work, Then Make It Better]], with an approval gate between the two halves.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
The approved rough cut is best presented as [[A Draft, Not a Verdict]], which invites structural feedback while it is still cheap. In software the same gate shows up as [[Define Contract Before Implementation]] and [[Spec-Driven Development]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Grill the Plan]] settles decisions before building, and this settles the build before decorating. [[Retention-Weighted Editing]] is one of the questions the foundation review should answer.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Some foundations can't be judged without a bit of polish; a rough cut with no sound can mislead a reviewer about pacing. [[Taste Is Earned Conviction]] suggests experienced makers sometimes need to finish a section to know if it works.
