# Your Mind Is for Having Ideas, Not Holding Them

## Idea
Mental clarity comes from getting every open loop out of your head and into a trusted system, so your mind is free to think instead of trying to remember.

## Definition
Every task you are trying to remember takes up attention in the background, whether or not you are working on it. The cure is to stop working from memory. If something can be written down, write it down: dump it into a to-do list or inbox you trust, and your head stops having to rehearse it. A few simple rules support this. **The two-minute rule:** if it takes under two minutes, do it now rather than recording it. **Done is better than perfect:** a finished thing closes a loop, while a perfect one in progress keeps it open. **Reset your space:** mess builds up by entropy, so instead of trying to stay tidy, build the habit of resetting. **Plan tomorrow the night before**, so you wake up to decisions already made.

## Source
Captured from my own reel on rules for mental clarity (October 2026).

The line and the two-minute rule are both David Allen's, from the *Getting Things Done* method (book 2001, revised 2015): if an action takes under two minutes, do it the moment it comes up. The quote "Your mind is for having ideas, not holding them" is widely attributed to Allen from his later talks and writing, though I couldn't confirm it in the 2001 text. The background-attention effect of unfinished tasks is the *Zeigarnik effect* (Bluma Zeigarnik, 1927). Masicampo & Baumeister (2011) showed that simply making a concrete plan for an unfinished goal reduces its intrusive pull.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the practical side of [[Mental Clarity and Meaning]]: clarity is something you build, and offloading is the first step. It also uses [[Bounded Rationality]], because attention is limited and every remembered task takes a share of it.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Once tasks are out of your head they can be sorted, which is where [[The Eisenhower Matrix]] and [[The Daily Highlight]] come in. For ideas rather than tasks, the trusted system becomes a [[Digital Garden]], and the first draft of a hard idea goes to [[Think on Paper First]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
"Done is better than perfect" is [[First Make It Work, Then Make It Better]] applied to your own task list, and the reset habit is a small piece of [[Environment Design]].

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
The trusted system only works if you trust it. A neglected inbox becomes one more thing to hold in your head, and capture can turn into a way of avoiding the work, a form of [[Toxic Productivity]]. The two-minute rule can also break up [[Deep Work]] if you apply it in the middle of a focus block.
