# JOMO (Joy of Missing Out)

## Idea
JOMO is the deliberate choice to not keep up with everything — finding peace in focused depth rather than anxious breadth.

## Definition
The Joy of Missing Out is the antidote to FOMO. Instead of scrambling to track every new tool, trend, or technique, JOMO says: pick one thing, get good at it, and let the hype cycle roll by. In the AI space, this is especially potent — every week brings new models, new frameworks, new workflows. The feeling that you're falling behind isn't real; it's a product. The AI industry sells that fear to drive engagement and purchases. But the models improve so fast that the hack you're panicking about today will be built into the tool itself in six months. Deep down, you already knew you couldn't keep up with all of it. That's not failure — that's freedom.

## Source
Coined by tech blogger Anil Dash in a July 2012 blog post titled "JOMO!", written shortly after the birth of his son, when being mostly offline made him realize he felt joy — not fear — at missing the usual digital noise. The post was inspired by Caterina Fake's earlier essay on FOMO. Danish philosopher Svend Brinkmann later gave the concept academic weight with his book *The Joy of Missing Out*. AI-specific framing from Anudeep Sharma's video on AI FOMO vs AI JOMO (June 2026).

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
JOMO lives under the broader question of [[Mental Clarity and Meaning]] — it's a strategy for protecting clarity by refusing to let the noise of every new thing crowd out what actually matters. It also connects to [[News is Weaponized]]: the AI hype cycle is just another form of weaponized information designed to make you feel inadequate.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Choosing JOMO means committing to [[Discipline Over Motivation]] — picking one tool and getting genuinely good at it instead of sampling thirty. This feeds directly into [[The Flywheel Concept]]: depth in one area compounds, while breadth across many resets the flywheel every week.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Hedonic Adaptation]] explains why the excitement of each new tool fades anyway — you adapt, the thrill dies, and you're chasing the next one. [[Calm and Peace]] is the emotional state JOMO produces: the quiet confidence of someone who chose their lane and stopped looking sideways.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
[[AI Removes the Barriers]] creates a tension — if AI makes it possible to do everything, shouldn't you try everything? JOMO says no: the ability to do everything doesn't mean you should. [[Curiosity]] is also a valid counterweight; some breadth keeps you from becoming narrow and stale. The trick is distinguishing genuine curiosity from manufactured urgency.
