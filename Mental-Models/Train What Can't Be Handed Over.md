# Train What Can't Be Handed Over

## Idea
As machines absorb every task that can be delegated, the practices worth investing in are the ones that build capacities you cannot hand over at all.

## Definition
Most advice about staying relevant alongside AI is a list of tools. This inverts it: look at what is being handed to machines, then deliberately train the things that remain stubbornly yours. The practices that qualify tend to be slow, physical, social or self-confronting — waiting on something that ignores you, reading people who are actively trying to be unreadable, publishing in your own name, sitting with a paper book, talking to someone a generation ahead. None of them are productive in the hour you do them. Each builds a capacity — patience, judgment, attention, foresight — that has no API.

## Source
Captured from my own reel on habits for the AI age (September 2026), which framed each habit as a disguised skill: fermentation is patience, publishing is clear thinking, poker is reading people, a paper book is attention.

The older lineage is Moravec's paradox — Hans Moravec's 1988 observation that what is hard for humans is easy for machines and what is effortless for humans is hardest to automate. The economist David Autor made the labour-market version of the argument through Polanyi's paradox: the tasks that resist automation are the ones whose rules we cannot articulate, which is exactly why they can't be specified and handed over.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This grows out of the question of what a person is actually for once the tools get good, which is the same question underneath [[AI as Amplifier]] — if AI magnifies whatever you already are, then what you are becomes the whole game. It also depends on seeing AI clearly rather than mystically, the correction [[AI Is a Tool, Not Magic]] makes.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Each undelegatable capacity turns into a concrete practice: calibration becomes a [[Prediction Journal]], real understanding becomes [[The Protégé Effect]], and judgment compounds into [[Taste Is Earned Conviction]]. Followed far enough it also reshapes a career, which is the move [[Tie Your Identity to How You Think]] describes.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
This is the behavioural sibling of [[Reading and Writing as Cognitive Gym]], which argues that AI removes mental exertion the way machines removed physical exertion and so we must now seek it deliberately. It shares its bias toward practice over study with [[We Learn by Doing, Not by Watching]].

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
The hedge here is that nobody knows which capacities are durable — every list of "things AI can't do" has been shortened by the next model, and [[Judging AI on a Version That Is Gone]] warns that these judgements rot fast. There is also a quietism risk: [[AI Removes the Barriers]] argues effort is now the only thing that matters, which suggests building things with the tools beats training in private for a future that may not arrive.
