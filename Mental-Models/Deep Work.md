# Deep Work

## Idea
Long stretches of distraction-free focus on hard problems produce most of the work that matters, and that kind of focus is getting rarer, which makes it more valuable.

## Definition
Deep work is concentrated effort on a cognitively demanding task with no distractions, pushing your abilities to their limit. Shallow work is the logistical stuff (email, quick replies, most meetings) that anyone could do while distracted. Newport's formula is that the quality of work produced equals time spent multiplied by intensity of focus. An hour of undivided attention beats three hours of checking messages in between. Every switch to something else leaves "attention residue" that drags on the next task, so fragmented days feel full but produce little. Deep work is a skill you train, not a mood you wait for. Newport describes four ways to schedule it: monastic (cut almost everything else out), bimodal (dedicate whole days or weeks), rhythmic (the same slot every day), and journalistic (grab whatever gaps appear).

## Source
Cal Newport, *Deep Work: Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World* (2016). "Attention residue" comes from Sophie Leroy's research (2009) on what happens when people switch tasks. The idea that deep concentration is a trainable skill echoes Anders Ericsson's deliberate-practice research. Newport extends the argument in [[Slow Productivity (Book)|Slow Productivity]].

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the answer to the [[Mere Urgency Effect]]: shallow, urgent tasks crowd out deep, important ones unless you protect time for the deep ones on purpose. [[The Eisenhower Matrix]] names the same split as "important but not urgent".

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Deep work needs a protected place and time, which is Kleon's [[Bliss Station]] and the one task of [[The Daily Highlight]]. Done for years at a sustainable pace, it becomes [[Slow Productivity]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
The [[Pareto Principle]] explains why it pays off: a small share of focused hours produces most of the valuable output. [[JOMO (Joy of Missing Out)]] is the emotional side, being at peace with what you miss while you're unreachable.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Some roles are mostly coordination, and for them responsiveness is the job, so "go dark for four hours" isn't an option. Deep work can also tip into [[Toxic Productivity]] when every hour is measured for its depth.
