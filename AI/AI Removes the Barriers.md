# AI Removes the Barriers

## Idea
AI hasn't made effort pointless — it's made effort the only thing that matters by removing every other barrier to creation.

## Definition
The common narrative is that AI does everything now, so what's the point of trying. The counterargument: if you can now build anything — video, images, code, writing, design — without needing years of specialized training in each domain, then the only variable left is whether you actually do the work. An engineer can build a marketing landing page. A writer can prototype an app. A student can produce professional-quality video. The barrier of "I don't know how" is largely gone. What remains is the barrier of "I haven't started." This makes the current moment the highest-leverage time in history to be a builder — not because building is easy, but because the prerequisites that used to gate-keep building have been dissolved.

## Source
Personal observation; crystallized while building a landing page as an engineer — a task outside one's trained domain made possible by AI tools.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This belongs to the broader story of the democratization of technology — each wave (the internet, smartphones, cloud computing, now AI) lowers the cost of creation and expands who can participate. The parent question is whether tools create creators or merely reveal them.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
If barriers are gone, then [[Show Your Work]] becomes more important than ever — the differentiator is no longer technical skill but the willingness to ship and iterate publicly. This also supercharges [[Volume Negates Luck]]: when each rep costs less (because AI accelerates it), you can do more reps, and volume negates luck even faster.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[We Learn by Doing, Not by Watching]] is the behavioral corollary — AI gives you the tools, but you still have to sit down and use them. [[The Parable of the Pottery Class]] resonates too: the students who made more pots made better pots, and AI just made it cheaper to make each pot.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
The counterargument is that if everyone can build, then quality and taste become the differentiators — which are precisely the things AI can't (yet) provide. There's also the risk of [[Toxic Productivity]]: the fact that you *can* now build everything doesn't mean you *should* — saying no is still a skill.
