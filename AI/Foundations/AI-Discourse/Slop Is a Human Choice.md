# Slop Is a Human Choice

## Idea
AI did not invent slop; it scaled it, so the blame belongs to the person who outsourced the thinking, not to the tool that produced the output.

## Definition
Low-effort content was on the internet long before AI: copy-paste blog posts, content farms, cheap outsourced work, the same thing reposted over and over. In every case the cause was the same. Someone handed their whole thinking, creative or problem-solving process to another person, system or tool, and shipped whatever came back. AI made that far cheaper and far larger, but it did not change where slop comes from. The same tools, used by someone who spent the effort up front on design decisions, prototyping and taste, and delegated only the grunt work, produce excellent websites, apps and videos. "Made with AI" says nothing about quality. "Made without thinking" says everything.

## Source
Captured from my own reel "Stop blaming AI for AI slop" (October 2026).

The word itself was popularised by Simon Willison's post "Slop is the new name for unwanted AI-generated content" (8 May 2024). He did not coin it: the term was already circulating online from around 2022. Merriam-Webster made "slop" its 2025 Word of the Year, defined as "digital content of low quality that is produced usually in quantity by means of artificial intelligence."

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This is [[AI as Amplifier]] seen from the output side: the tool magnifies whatever effort went in, including none. It also follows from [[AI Removes the Barriers]], because when production costs nothing, the effort upstream is the only thing that tells good work from bad.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
The fix is upstream, in [[Clarity In, Clarity Out]] and [[Hand Off the Code, Not the Understanding]]: keep the thinking, delegate the production. For creators it argues for showing the process, as [[Building in Public]] does, because visible effort is the clearest signal that something isn't slop.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits next to [[Dead Internet Theory]], which is what the internet looks like if slop wins on volume, and [[Good Theft vs Bad Theft]], which draws the same line between transforming a source and just repackaging it.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Scale is not neutral. When slop costs nothing to make, it can flood search results, feeds and review queues faster than any human can curate them, so "blame the person" alone underestimates the effect on the whole system. That is a [[Tragedy of the Commons]] problem, not just a matter of individual ethics.
