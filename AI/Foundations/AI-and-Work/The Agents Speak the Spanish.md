# The Agents Speak the Spanish

## Idea
Writing syntax was always a language skill, like speaking Spanish; now that agents speak it, the engineer's job is deciding what is worth saying.

## Definition
Programming syntax is a real technical skill, but it is a translation skill, the same kind as learning Spanish or German. Agents now do that translation, so letting them code has not made the work easier. It has removed the part that was easy to mistake for the whole job. What's left is all decisions: what are the business requirements, what are the technical ones, how fast, how reliable, at what cost, and where it should go in the codebase. Codebase memory, knowing which class inherits from which, matters less. System-level thinking matters more: what goes in, what comes out, and how to close the gap between code that works and what the user actually wants. Each of those calls is small, and no agent will catch one you get wrong. The work hasn't become less technical. If anything, the share that is real engineering has gone up.

## Source
Captured from two of my own reels: "Everybody's an engineer now" (November 2026) and "The day I realised my workday had changed" (October 2026). Figures checked:

- At Google Cloud Next in April 2026, Sundar Pichai said **75% of Google's new code** is AI-generated and approved by engineers. The figure was "more than a quarter" in October 2024 and around half by late 2025. It refers to new code, not the whole codebase.
- Temporal's *State of Development 2026* (about 550 US and UK engineers, spring 2026) found **80.8%** use AI agents daily or more, up from 47.3% a year earlier.

The "English is the new programming language" framing is Andrej Karpathy's, from a 2023 post: "The hottest new programming language is English."

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It extends [[English Is the Programming Language]]: if the interface is plain sentences, the scarce skill moves from writing to deciding. It is the engineering version of [[Tie Your Identity to How You Think]].

**Paths** — *where this leads*
The decisions it describes are what [[Engineering Starts After the Demo]] means by real engineering. Making them well depends on [[Hand Off the Code, Not the Understanding]], and recording them, as [[Spec-Driven Development]] and [[Architecture Decision Records (ADR)]] do, keeps them from being lost.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits near [[Direct and Check]], which is the same shift described as two skills, and [[Systems Thinking]], which is the "inputs, outputs, gaps" habit applied to software.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Fluency still matters for checking: you can't catch a subtle bug in a language you can only half read. So "the agents speak it" is true for writing and less true for reviewing, which is the gap [[Never Grade Your Own Homework]] is about.
