# Ship Reps, Not Grunt Work

## Idea
The old path to senior was doing the boring work long enough; that work is exactly what agents do best now, so the new path is shipping many complete things and directing agents to get there.

## Definition
Juniors used to spend months testing a senior's features, reading logs and slowly learning the codebase, on the theory that if you did the boring work long enough you would one day be senior. Agents now do that work faster, at scale, and without tiring, so doing it by hand is no longer training. It is just slow. The entry rung really is thinner: hiring growth is going to senior roles. But the same tool that is taking the junior work also compresses the learning curve. A junior can now get through many complete cycles of building, shipping, breaking and fixing in the time one used to take. Directing a team of agents to a result is also an early version of what seniors always did, which is managing work rather than doing every piece of it. So hand the grunt work to an agent, and spend the saved time on finished projects.

## Source
Captured from two of my own reels: on how juniors become senior (November 2026) and on the jobs of the future (October 2026). The data checks out:

- **Indeed Hiring Lab (July 2026):** US software development postings rose about 15% from late February 2025, roughly when Claude Code launched, while overall postings fell about 7%. Of the growth from May 2025 to May 2026, 71% came from senior roles and 37% from postings with "AI" in the title (the two groups overlap). Postings are still well below February 2020, and Indeed itself calls this correlation, not causation.
- **Stanford, "Canaries in the Coal Mine" (Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, August 2025):** ADP payroll data show a 13% *relative* decline in employment for 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed jobs, such as software development.
- **WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025:** a survey of over 1,000 employers, projecting 170M jobs created and 92M displaced between 2025 and 2030.

The idea that reps beat time served has an older lineage in deliberate-practice research (Anders Ericsson) and in the idea that output volume, not tenure, drives skill.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is [[We Learn by Doing, Not by Watching]] with the cost of doing pushed close to zero, and [[The Parable of the Pottery Class]] with agents as the clay: whoever makes more finished pots learns faster.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Shipping reps only builds seniority if each rep leaves understanding behind, which is why it depends on [[Hand Off the Code, Not the Understanding]]. The skills it trains are [[Direct and Check]], the two parts of the job that get more valuable as tasks get cheap.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits beside [[Volume Negates Luck]] and [[Learning Agility]]: more attempts means more chances to get lucky, and more practice at the meta-skill of learning something new quickly.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Some of the grunt work *was* the training. Reading logs is how you learn what failure looks like, and an agent that does it for you can also take that lesson away, which is the warning in [[Train What Can't Be Handed Over]]. And reps without feedback from a senior can just repeat the same mistakes faster.
