# Hand Off the Code, Not the Understanding

## Idea
The same AI makes some people sharper and others duller, and the difference is whether they hand it the work or hand it the part they were supposed to understand.

## Definition
Delegating the output is fine. Delegating the understanding is how you get dumber. Before an agent writes anything, ask it what the system does, what goes in, what's wrong, how it should behave, and for a new feature, where it goes and how it fits. After it does something you haven't seen before, ask it to explain what it did, and write that down. Over weeks, the things it does that are new to you get fewer and fewer, because what it does has become what you know. Purely mechanical work, like reformatting a spreadsheet, renaming a hundred files, a regex you will never need again, or turning a PDF table into Excel, can be handed off whole. The test: if someone asked how the last thing AI built for you works, could you answer? If not, you were not in the driver's seat.

## Source
Captured from two of my own reels: "AI is making some people sharper and others dumber" (November 2026) and "Help AI help you" (October 2026).

The research backs it up, with caveats. Lee et al. (Microsoft Research and Carnegie Mellon, CHI 2025) surveyed 319 knowledge workers about 936 tasks. Higher confidence in AI went with less critical thinking, while higher self-confidence went with more, and effort shifted from producing work to verifying and overseeing it. Kosmyna et al.'s "Your Brain on ChatGPT" (MIT Media Lab, June 2025; small sample, not peer reviewed) coined "cognitive debt". Essay-writers who used an LLM showed the weakest neural connectivity and often could not quote what they had just "written". The underlying concept is *cognitive offloading* (Risko & Gilbert, 2016).

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the practice behind [[AI as Amplifier]]: if the tool magnifies what you bring, then what you understand is the thing being magnified. It also applies [[The Protégé Effect]] in reverse, because asking the AI to explain forces you to rebuild the explanation yourself.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Kept understanding is what makes [[Direct and Check]] possible, and it compounds like interest. That is why writing each lesson into a [[Digital Garden]] beats trying every new tool, and why [[Ship Reps, Not Grunt Work]] only builds seniority when each rep leaves something behind.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits beside [[Train What Can't Be Handed Over]] and [[Read the Long Version]], both of which protect the thinking while the doing is delegated. [[Ask It to Ask You]] is the same move pointed the other way, with the AI questioning you.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Understanding everything does not scale: at some point you have to trust a module you have never read, the way you trust a compiler. [[Selective Intelligence]] is the counterweight, because knowing what you can safely *not* understand is part of the skill.
