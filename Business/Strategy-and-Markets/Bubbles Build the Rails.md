# Bubbles Build the Rails

## Idea
A transformative technology can be a financial bubble and still change everything, because the crash wipes out the investors, not the infrastructure.

## Definition
"Is AI a bubble?" and "Is AI transformative?" are separate questions, and both answers can be yes. Money pours into a new technology faster than real customers can pay for it, and some of that money moves in a circle. One company invests in another, which spends it on the first company's product, and both report growth. At some point the spending has to be justified by real people paying real money, and if it isn't, the valuations collapse. But the tracks, the fibre and the data centres built during the frenzy stay in the ground, cheap now, for the next wave of builders to use. The bubble pays for the build-out, and the crash hands it over.

## Source
Captured from my own reel "Is AI a bubble?" (October 2026).

The framework is Carlota Perez's *Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital: The Dynamics of Bubbles and Golden Ages* (2002). Each technological revolution has an *installation period* driven by speculative finance, which ends in a crash. That crash is the turning point into a *deployment period*, when the infrastructure gets put to productive use. Her examples include Britain's Railway Mania, which peaked in 1845 and crashed in 1847, and the dot-com crash of 2000.

The capture's circular-money example checks out. In February 2026 Nvidia put $30B into OpenAI as part of a $110B round. OpenAI in turn committed to about 5GW of Nvidia systems, so much of the money flows back as chip orders. Critics call this circular financing; Nvidia disputes the label.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
Circular financing is a [[Reinforcing Feedback Loop]] running on capital instead of customers, and like every reinforcing loop it eventually meets [[Limits to Growth]]. Here the limit is end-user revenue, which is what [[Unit Economics]] measures.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
For a career, the crash is when jobs tied to the old way go and new ones appear. That makes adding to your bundle, as [[A Job Is a Bundle of Tasks]] puts it, a better bet than trying to time the market. For builders, the deployment period is the opportunity: infrastructure someone else overpaid for becomes cheap to build on.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It pairs with [[Judging AI on a Version That Is Gone]]: both warn against reading the technology's long-run value off a single snapshot, whether that snapshot is a weak model or an inflated valuation.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
"The rails stay" is survivorship talk. Not every bubble leaves useful infrastructure, and the people who lose their savings or jobs in the crash do not get the golden age. Perez's cycle is also a pattern drawn from five revolutions, and [[Every Model Is a Simplification]].
