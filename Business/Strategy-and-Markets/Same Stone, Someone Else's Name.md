# Same Stone, Someone Else's Name

## Idea
Making the thing is the low-margin part; whoever puts a name on it and sells it captures the value.

## Definition
A country, company or person can be the backbone of an industry and still be cheap to replace, because services are paid for by the hour and brands are paid for by trust. Potassium alum (*phitkri*) has been rubbed on skin after a shave and used to clear muddy water in Indian homes for generations, and India makes and exports it. An American put the same mineral salt in a box, called it Crystal, and sold it as a deodorant around the world. Same stone, someone else's name. When automation makes the service work cheaper, the side that only supplies labour loses its edge first. The side that owns products, brands and original ideas keeps its pricing power.

## Source
Captured from my own reel on branding (November 2026). The Crystal story is real, with one correction. In 1984, Jerry Rosenblatt came across alum mineral salt on a trip to France, asked the factory for palm-sized pieces, and founded French Transit Ltd. in California to sell them as Crystal Body Deodorant. He did not discover that alum works as a deodorant, which people have known for centuries; he imported it and branded it.

The underlying model is the **smile curve**, proposed by Acer founder Stan Shih around 1992. Plot value added along the value chain and you get a smile: high at the ends (R&D and design on one side, brand, marketing and service on the other), low in the middle where manufacturing and assembly sit.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This is [[A Job Is a Bundle of Tasks]] applied to a whole economy: a services sector built on tasks that AI is making cheap is a thin bundle. It is also the logic behind [[Unit Economics]], since margin comes from what you can charge, not from how hard you work.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
At the personal scale it becomes [[Plant Seeds, Don't Climb Ladders]], where a personal brand replaces the CV. For anything built in public it argues for owning the audience relationship, as [[Building in Public]] does, rather than renting it.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits near [[Metcalfe's Law]] and [[Matthew Effect]]: once a name is attached and spreading, recognition compounds, and the original maker finds it ever harder to compete on the product alone.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Brands without substance decay, and the reverse is true too: the middle of the smile curve can be a fortress when it is hard to copy, as TSMC shows. "More products, fewer services" is a direction, not a rule; plenty of service businesses earn product-like margins once they own a niche.
