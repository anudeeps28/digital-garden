# Plant Seeds, Don't Climb Ladders

## Idea
When skills expire faster than you can climb a ladder, careers grow like a garden instead: many small bets, one deep root, and a name people remember.

## Definition
The old script was linear: good school, good degree, good job, climb the ladder, retire. It assumed the skill that got you onto a rung would still be worth something by the time you reached the next one. That stops being true when skills expire quickly and AI makes many listed skills happen on their own. The replacement is less like a ladder and more like a garden, built on three moves. **Build a personal brand, not a résumé:** people hire who they remember, and they remember what you have shown them. **Plant more seeds:** be in more rooms and stay shallow on many skills, while keeping the willingness to go deep on one when it matters. **Invest in what doesn't fit on a résumé:** problem-solving, communication and judgment. The biggest flex stops being the title and becomes the range of things you can grow into.

## Source
Captured from my own reel "The career ladder is dead" (October 2026).

The structure has two older names. **T-shaped skills**, broad across many areas and deep in one, is usually traced to David Guest's 1991 article in *The Independent* (the idea circulated at McKinsey in the 1980s) and was popularised by Tim Brown of IDEO. The **career lattice**, which lets careers move sideways and diagonally instead of only up, comes from Cathleen Benko and Molly Anderson of Deloitte, *The Corporate Lattice* (2010).

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the career strategy that follows from [[Learning Agility]] and [[Tie Your Identity to How You Think]]: if every specific skill expires, invest in the ability to grow new ones and in an identity that outlives them.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
The personal-brand move is [[Building in Public]] in practice, and the economics of [[Same Stone, Someone Else's Name]] at the scale of one person. Planting many seeds is a way of using [[Volume Negates Luck]] and the [[Power Law Distribution]]: a few of the bets will return most of the value.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits near [[A Job Is a Bundle of Tasks]], since each seed adds a task to your bundle, and near [[80,000 Hours Career Guide]], which takes the same "explore, then exploit" approach to a whole career.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Shallow on everything can turn into deep on nothing, and the "attitude to go deep" only counts if you actually do it somewhere, as [[Taste Is Earned Conviction]] insists. Ladders also aren't dead everywhere: medicine, law and many large firms still promote by rung.
