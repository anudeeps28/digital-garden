# Assumption Register

## Idea
List every assumption a new venture rests on, rate how much each one matters and how well it's supported, and decide in advance how you'll test it and what result would mean stop.

## Definition
Every business plan is a stack of guesses that look like facts. An **assumption register** pulls them out into a table. Each row has the assumption itself, a **severity** (dealbreaker, significant, or minor: what happens if it's wrong), a **category** (demand: do people want it; behaviour: will they actually do the thing; feasibility: can it be built and run), and the current **evidence strength**. The crucial step is the last one: for each dealbreaker, write down the validation method and the go/no-go threshold *before* seeing any results. For example, "at least 8 of 20 interviewed users describe this problem unprompted" is set first, then the interviews happen. Pre-registering the bar stops the most common failure in early-stage validation, which is quietly moving the goalposts until whatever came back counts as success.

## Source
Rita Gunther McGrath and Ian C. MacMillan, "Discovery-Driven Planning", *Harvard Business Review* (1995), which argued that new ventures should plan to convert assumptions into knowledge at milestones rather than be judged on projections, and included an explicit assumptions checklist. The test-first spirit was later popularised by Eric Ries's *The Lean Startup* (2011). Pre-registration itself is borrowed from scientific practice.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is [[Critical Thinking Skills]] made into a spreadsheet, and the pre-registered threshold is the same discipline as a [[Prediction Journal]]: write the forecast down before reality can edit your memory of it.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Demand rows are usually tested first, and feasibility rows feed straight into [[Unit Economics]]. Decisions the register settles are worth recording as [[Explicit Non-Goals]] when the answer is no.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[Tag Claims as Cited or Assumed]] is the same separation of evidence from belief at the level of single sentences, and [[Grill the Plan]] is a good way to surface the rows in the first place.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Founders often succeed by acting before the evidence is in, the energy of [[Delusional and Disciplined]]. A register run too strictly can kill ideas whose demand only appears once the product exists.
