# Tie Your Identity to How You Think

## Idea
A career anchored to one executable skill is automatable; one anchored to judgment — deciding which problem is worth solving — is not.

## Definition
The risk is not being bad at your skill. It is being only your skill. If your professional identity is "I write the SQL" or "I cut the video", then the day a model does that competently, there is nothing left of the role. The defensible position sits one level up: knowing which problem actually matters, what good looks like, and what to build next. Critically, the answer is not to out-learn the model — that race is lost on volume before it starts. It is to hold the layer the model is not operating at, and to let the specific skills underneath you be replaceable without the identity going with them.

## Source
Captured from my own reel on reinventing yourself (September 2026), written against the backdrop of frontier models going from a 2021 cutoff and a few thousand tokens of context to a million tokens and live web access in roughly two years.

The career-strategy version is older. Scott Adams coined the "talent stack": rather than becoming the best in the world at one thing, become top-25% at several that rarely appear together, since the combination is what turns out to be rare. The economics underneath is David Autor's framing that automation takes *tasks*, not jobs — which means a role survives in proportion to how much of it is judgment that cannot be specified.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This is the career consequence of [[AI as Amplifier]]: if the tool magnifies whatever you bring, then what you bring has to be something worth magnifying, and a single executable skill isn't it. It assumes the world described in [[Depth Is Cheap]], where specialised depth can be borrowed on demand and the expensive part is how you think.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
In practice this means getting good at the ill-defined work rather than the well-defined work, which is the skill [[Managing Ambiguity]] treats as the real ladder. It changes what you optimise for in a job, in the direction [[How to Make More Money in Your Job]] argues, and over a whole career it is the bet [[80,000 Hours Career Guide]] asks you to make deliberately.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits right beside [[Learning Agility]], which makes the same wager from the other end — every specific skill expires, so the meta-skill of acquiring them is the durable asset. It is the career-shaped instance of [[Train What Can't Be Handed Over]].

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
The honest objection is that judgment without craft is just opinion: you cannot tell which problem is worth solving in a domain you have never built in, which is why [[Taste Is Earned Conviction]] insists the judgment is a residue of having made a great many things. Generic "thinking skills" with no substrate is also how people end up in the trap [[Selective Intelligence]] points at — clever in general, useful nowhere in particular.
