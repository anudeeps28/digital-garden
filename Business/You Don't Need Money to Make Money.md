# You Don't Need Money to Make Money

## Idea
The real prerequisites for creating wealth aren't capital — they're a marketable skill, the right mindset, and the willingness to execute.

## Definition
The conventional wisdom says you need money to make money. The contrarian view: what you actually need is a skill someone will pay for, the mindset to see opportunities rather than obstacles, and the discipline to execute consistently rather than perpetually planning. Money follows value creation, and value creation follows skill development. This is why broke founders can build successful companies while well-funded ones fail — capital is an accelerant, not a foundation. The formula is sequential: develop a skill that solves real problems, cultivate the mindset to spot where that skill is needed, then execute relentlessly.

## Source
Raj Shamani, via @thinkwithshamani on Instagram; a common entrepreneurship maxim.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This belongs to the bootstrapping school of entrepreneurship — the belief that resourcefulness beats resources. It's also a restatement of the age-old tension between capital and labor, coming down firmly on the side of human capital as the primary asset.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
If skill is the foundation, then deliberate skill acquisition becomes the highest-return investment you can make. This connects to [[Volume Negates Luck]] — the more you practice and ship, the more skilled you become, and the more opportunities you create without needing capital.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
[[AI Removes the Barriers]] is the modern amplifier of this truth — AI reduces the cost of execution, which means the skill-mindset-execution formula works even faster now. [[The Flywheel Concept]] describes the mechanics: each execution rep builds both skill and reputation, which makes the next rep easier.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
The phrase can become toxic if taken too literally — systemic barriers (access, education, geography, discrimination) are real, and "just have the right mindset" can dismiss structural disadvantage. [[Your Environment Is Your Destiny]] reminds us that starting conditions do matter, even if they're not deterministic.
