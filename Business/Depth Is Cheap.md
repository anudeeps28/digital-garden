# Depth Is Cheap

## Idea
AI made specialized depth cheap to borrow — what's expensive now is how you think and whether you move at all.

## Definition
The easiest way to become replaceable in the next decade is to get really good at exactly one thing. Specialization made sense when you learned one skill at twenty-two and did it until sixty. But now someone with a fraction of the experience, using AI well, gets to ninety percent of what a specialist produces. The depth that used to take six years to earn can be borrowed in an afternoon. So if depth isn't the moat, two things are: (1) how you actually think — given a problem, how your brain takes it apart, how far in you're willing to go, how you connect dots in uncertainty; (2) whether you move at all — because in a world where you can survive doing nothing, the people who just do things, badly, early, repeatedly, walk past everyone still deciding.

## Source
Anudeep Sharma's content (Instagram, August 2026). The observation builds on the broader "AI commoditizes knowledge" discourse and echoes Naval Ravikant's idea that specific knowledge combined with leverage creates value, not expertise alone.

**Clash** — [[The Paradox of Freedom]] argues the opposite direction — that depth still matters enormously because it gives you the judgment to direct AI effectively. The resolution: depth in *thinking* is expensive; depth in *information* is cheap.
