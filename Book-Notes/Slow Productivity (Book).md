# Slow Productivity

## Summary
Cal Newport's 2024 book, subtitled *The Lost Art of Accomplishment Without Burnout*, argues that knowledge work went wrong when it started measuring productivity by visible busyness, what he calls **pseudo-productivity**. He draws on writers, scientists and artists who produced important work over long periods and proposes three principles: do fewer things, work at a natural pace, and obsess over quality.

## Key Ideas
- **Pseudo-productivity**: using visible activity (emails, meetings, quick replies) as a proxy for real work, which rewards being busy over producing results.
- **Do fewer things**: cut active commitments so each one gets real attention, because the overhead of every extra commitment eats the time that could make the others good.
- **Work at a natural pace**: vary intensity across days, weeks and seasons instead of sprinting all the time. Important work has always taken longer than it looks.
- **Obsess over quality**: focus on getting the work excellent, which in turn justifies saying no to everything else.
