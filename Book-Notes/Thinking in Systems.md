# Thinking in Systems

## Summary
Donella Meadows' primer on how systems work, published in 2008 after her death in 2001 and edited by Diana Wright. Its argument is that a system's behavior comes from its own structure (stocks, flows and feedback loops), so recurring problems are solved by changing that structure, not by blaming events or people. The book moves from the basic building blocks, through the ways systems surprise and trap us, to where to intervene and how to live with systems you cannot fully control.

## Key Ideas

**Part One: System Structure and Behavior**
- **Ch 1, The Basics.** A system is [[Elements, Interconnections, and Purpose]], and [[The Purpose of a System Is What It Does]], not what it claims. Its state lives in [[Stocks and Flows]] and it steers through a [[Balancing Feedback Loop]] or grows through a [[Reinforcing Feedback Loop]]. Many of its connections are information, which is why [[Information Flows Shape Behavior]].
- **Ch 2, A Brief Visit to the Systems Zoo.** A catalog of common systems sorted by how their stocks behave over time: settling to equilibrium, growing then collapsing, or oscillating. [[Delays Cause Oscillation]], every growth story ends in [[Limits to Growth]], growth is capped by the current [[Limiting Factor]], and resources run out differently depending on whether they are [[Stock-Limited vs Flow-Limited Resources|stock-limited or flow-limited]].
- **Ch 3, Why Systems Work So Well.** Resilience, self-organization and hierarchy are why systems persist and can be more than the sum of their parts. *(Not yet covered in my notes.)*

**Part Two: Systems and Us**
- **Ch 4, Why Systems Surprise Us.** [[Every Model Is a Simplification]], we are [[Linear Minds in a Nonlinear World]], limiting factors shift as systems grow, and actors work within [[Bounded Rationality]].
- **Ch 5, System Traps and Opportunities.** Eight recurring failure patterns, each with a way out:
  1. [[Policy Resistance]]: competing goals cancel out every push
  2. [[Tragedy of the Commons]]: individual gain, shared depletion
  3. [[Drift to Low Performance]]: standards erode toward recent results
  4. [[Escalation]]: retaliation spirals
  5. [[Success to the Successful]]: winners get the means to keep winning
  6. [[Shifting the Burden]]: quick fixes weaken the system's own ability to cope
  7. [[Rule Beating]]: obeying the letter while defeating the spirit
  8. [[Seeking the Wrong Goal]]: optimizing the metric instead of the outcome

**Part Three: Creating Change**
- **Ch 6, Leverage Points.** [[Leverage Points]] rank interventions from weakest (numbers) to strongest (goals and paradigms).
- **Ch 7, Living in a World of Systems.** You cannot control complex systems, but you can learn to work with them: [[Dancing with Systems]].

**Related idea (not from the book):** the [[Matthew Effect]], [[Power Law Distribution]] and [[Metcalfe's Law]] explain how [[Success to the Successful]] produces inequality in wealth, attention and platforms.
